Pope Leo XIV told reporters on Monday that worries about artificial intelligence running amok are not “fake news” and must be taken seriously, directly challenging President Donald Trump’s description of the same concerns as a “hoax.”

## Key Operational Details

During a press conference aboard his return flight from Metz, France, the pontiff was asked what steps the United States and China should take to prevent AI from posing catastrophic risks. He replied that the issue requires a sit‑down discussion and cannot be ignored, emphasizing that his first encyclical is devoted to AI and the protection of humanity.

President Trump, a vocal proponent of maintaining U.S. leadership in AI, has repeatedly dismissed safety concerns as a hoax and framed any effort to curb AI development as a “conspiracy” by political opponents. His remarks stand in stark contrast to the Pope’s call for urgent dialogue.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, whose firm is among several AI giants urging a slowdown, warned that within six to twelve months AI could orchestrate a swarm of agents capable of taking over the entire internet. Trump said on Sunday he plans a late dinner with Amodei but has not yet commented on the CEO’s warning.

The exchange highlights a growing rift between religious leadership urging precaution and political rhetoric emphasizing competition. While the Pope’s encyclical marks a historic papal foray into technology ethics, Trump’s stance reflects a broader governmental focus on strategic advantage over regulatory caution.

## Stakeholder Impact & Next Steps

Pope Leo’s remarks may amplify calls for international cooperation on AI safety, especially as the United States and China are singled out as key actors. Trump’s scheduled dinner with Amodei suggests a potential, though unconfirmed, dialogue between political and industry leaders, but no concrete policy actions have been announced.
