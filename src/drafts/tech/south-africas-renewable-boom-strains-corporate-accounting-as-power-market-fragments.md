In the first half of 2026, South Africa saw 17 independent power producer projects reach commercial operation, adding 1.92 GW of capacity – the highest half‑year addition on record and already surpassing the previous full‑year peak of 1.47 GW set in 2016. At the same time, Nersa logged 124 generation facilities in the first quarter of the 2026/27 financial year, representing 804 MW of capacity and R20.2 billion of investment. The surge is stretching the accounting systems of organisations that now buy power from a rapidly diversifying market.

## Policy Shift and Market Structure

The accounting pressure stems from two regulatory reforms. In 2021 the generation‑licensing threshold was raised from 1 MW to 100 MW, and in January 2023 amendments to Schedule 2 of the Electricity Regulation Act removed licensing for facilities that wheel power across third‑party networks, replacing permission with simple registration. The reforms opened the market to a flood of smaller and mid‑size projects, but many corporate buyers have not yet updated their financial and risk‑management frameworks to reflect the new supply chain dynamics.

## Pooled Wheeling Pilot

In May 2026 Growthpoint, trader Etana Energy and the City of Cape Town announced South Africa’s first pooled wheeling of renewable electricity across multiple municipal properties. Power from the Boston hydroelectric plant near Clarens – co‑owned by Serengeti Energy and Growthpoint – is wheeled into Eskom’s network and then allocated across five Growthpoint buildings rather than being tied to a single site. Growthpoint has signalled an ambition to extend the model to more than 30 of its city‑based properties, illustrating how large tenants are experimenting with shared renewable supply.

## Trader Dominance

Discovery Green CEO Andre Nepgen estimated that roughly 80 % of newly closed private renewable generation over the preceding year was channelled through traders instead of direct bilateral contracts. The shift means that corporate buyers are now purchasing from a market intermediary rather than negotiating directly with generators, adding a layer of complexity to contract accounting, price risk assessment and compliance reporting.

## Real‑Time Monitoring Technology

Advances in cheaper sensors, low‑power wide‑area networks and cloud platforms now enable organisations to monitor electricity consumption in near‑real time, replacing the legacy practice of manual monthly meter reads. This technological upgrade provides the data granularity needed to reconcile actual usage with the more fluid, trader‑mediated supply contracts that are emerging across the country’s power landscape.

## Stakeholder Impact & Next Steps

The convergence of rapid capacity growth, regulatory liberalisation and trader‑driven procurement is prompting finance teams to overhaul accounting processes, integrate real‑time consumption data and reassess risk models. Growthpoint’s plan to roll the pooled wheeling scheme out to over 30 properties signals a broader industry move toward shared renewable sourcing, while corporations will need to align their bookkeeping and reporting systems with these evolving market structures.
