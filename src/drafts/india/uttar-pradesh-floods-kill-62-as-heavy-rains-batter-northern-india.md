At least 62 people have died and 46 sustained injuries after three days of relentless rainfall triggered widespread flooding across Uttar Pradesh, state officials confirmed Monday. More than 1,000 houses were reported damaged as rivers and drainage systems overflowed in multiple districts, cutting off villages and submerging low‑lying neighborhoods.

## Rescue Operations and Public Advisories

Rescue teams deployed boats and heavy equipment to evacuate residents from inundated areas, relocating them to temporary shelters on higher ground. Authorities ordered schools closed Monday in affected zones, including the state capital Lucknow, and urged the public to avoid unnecessary travel through waterlogged roads. A formal advisory warned citizens to stay clear of fast‑flowing rivers, streams and open drains, which remain hazardous despite receding water levels in some sectors.

## Meteorological Outlook

The downpour was driven by a low‑pressure system over the Bay of Bengal that has begun to weaken, according to Atul Kumar Singh, senior scientist at the Regional Meteorological Centre in Lucknow. Singh said rainfall intensity should ease from Monday, though light to moderate showers may persist through Tuesday across eastern and western parts of the state. The forecast offers a narrow window for relief crews to reach isolated communities and assess structural damage to homes and infrastructure.

## Regional Spillover: Odisha and Nepal

Flooding extended beyond Uttar Pradesh. In the eastern state of Odisha, officials said more than 75,000 people were moved to safer locations as rivers breached embankments. Neighbouring Nepal reported 21 weather‑related deaths between Thursday and Sunday, with five people still missing and five injured. Floods and landslides damaged 194 houses there, compounding a humanitarian crisis that began in August when catastrophic floods killed more than 1,400 people and left nearly 6,000 unaccounted for.

## Mounting Recovery Challenges

The dual disaster — fresh flooding in the Himalayan foothills and the lingering aftermath of August’s devastation — stretches Nepal’s disaster management capacity. In Uttar Pradesh, the scale of housing damage suggests a prolonged displacement for thousands of families, while agricultural losses in the fertile Gangetic plain could affect rural livelihoods well beyond the monsoon season. Coordination between state agencies, the National Disaster Response Force and local administrations will determine how quickly basic services are restored.
