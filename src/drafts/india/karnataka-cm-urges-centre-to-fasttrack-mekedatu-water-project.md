According to the India Today article published on 28 September 2026, titled "Karnataka urges Centre to fast‑track clearance for Mekedatu, other water projects," Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar appealed directly to the Union government to accelerate clearance for the Mekedatu water project and additional water initiatives. The statement signals the state’s urgency in securing central approval to move forward with critical water infrastructure. Shivakumar’s remarks were reported as a formal request to the central authorities, emphasizing the need for swift action on pending permissions.

## Key Operational Details

Shivakumar’s appeal specifically targeted the central authorities, urging them to expedite the pending approvals that are essential for moving the Mekedatu scheme and additional water projects forward. The article notes that the clearances are required to advance the project timeline, indicating that the state views the central government’s response as pivotal for implementation. By highlighting the pending nature of the approvals, the CM underscored the procedural bottlenecks that currently delay the water development agenda.

## Background & Context

The Mekedatu water project forms part of a broader set of water‑related initiatives that Karnataka considers vital for regional water security. The state’s push for fast‑track clearance reflects the growing pressure on inter‑state water management and the need for coordinated federal support. While the article does not detail the technical specifications of the projects, it establishes that the CM’s request encompasses both the flagship Mekedatu undertaking and related schemes, suggesting a comprehensive approach to addressing water resource challenges.

## Stakeholder Impact & Next Steps

The report does not detail any immediate actions taken by the Centre following the appeal, and no further official statements have been released as of the article’s publication. This absence of a response indicates that the central government has not yet communicated its stance on the clearance request. Stakeholders, including farmers and water management agencies in Karnataka, remain awaiting a decision that could unlock funding and construction activities.

The appeal highlights the state’s reliance on central coordination for large‑scale water infrastructure. If the Centre accelerates the clearance process, it could set a precedent for other regional water projects seeking federal support. Conversely, continued delays may intensify pressure on Karnataka’s water planning and fuel political discourse on federal‑state cooperation in resource development.
