X, the social‑media platform formerly known as Twitter, disclosed that its security team had uncovered a large‑scale bot operation originating from China. The operation comprised roughly 200,000 accounts that were systematically posting content that criticised artificial‑intelligence data‑centres, often framing them as harmful or wasteful. According to X, the posts were amplified through coordinated retweets and replies, reaching a broad audience of users who followed the accounts for technology news. The platform said it had taken down the entire network after confirming that the accounts violated its policies against coordinated inauthentic behaviour. The removal was carried out in a single action, and the accounts were permanently suspended, preventing further dissemination of the anti‑AI narrative.

The bot farm appeared to be part of a broader effort to shape public perception of AI infrastructure, a sector that has drawn increasing scrutiny over its energy consumption and environmental impact. While X did not attribute the operation to any specific organization, the scale and language patterns suggested a coordinated campaign likely linked to actors in mainland China. X’s security team said the detection was the result of ongoing automated monitoring tools combined with manual review, highlighting the platform’s continued focus on combating misinformation and foreign influence operations.

X’s statement did not include details about the exact content of the posts, but observers noted that the messages often cited alleged data‑centre waste, privacy concerns, and geopolitical risks associated with AI development. By removing the accounts, X aims to limit the reach of coordinated disinformation that could sway public opinion or policy discussions around emerging technologies.
