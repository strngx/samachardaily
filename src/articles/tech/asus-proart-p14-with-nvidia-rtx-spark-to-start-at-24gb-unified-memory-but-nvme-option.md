Detailed specifications for ASUS's forthcoming ProArt P14, a creator-class laptop built around NVIDIA's RTX Spark processor, have surfaced ahead of launch, outlining both base and top-end hardware tiers. The Taiwanese manufacturer has confirmed that even the entry configuration will ship with 24GB of LPDDR5X unified memory, an aggressive floor for the category that puts the machine on more equal footing with Apple's unified-memory MacBook Pro lineup rather than the 16GB baselines common across Windows thin-and-light workstations.

The trade-offs, however, begin at storage. ASUS is reportedly equipping the ProArt P14 with only a single M.2 NVMe slot, capping user upgrades to a single drive replacement rather than the dual-slot configurations offered on rivals such as the Dell XPS 15 and Lenovo ThinkPad P-series. Worse for performance-oriented buyers, the available slot does not support the newer PCIe NVMe Gen 5 standard, meaning the laptop will rely on the Gen 4 bus and forgo the sequential read and write speeds now reaching above 12,000 MB/s on flagship SSDs from Samsung, Crucial, and other NAND vendors.

Charging flexibility is another notable compromise in the leaked spec sheet. ASUS has opted against including a proprietary charging brick ecosystem, instead relying on a standardized USB-C power delivery solution. While that aligns the ProArt P14 with the broader USB-C PD ecosystem used across ultrabooks and modern tablets, it likely caps sustained power delivery below the wattage of traditional barrel-jack adapters, which could throttle performance during extended GPU-accelerated rendering or AI-inference workloads on the RTX Spark's neural processing units.

Positioned against the recently refreshed MacBook Pro M4 lineup and Dell's Precision workstations, the ProArt P14's 24GB memory floor signals ASUS's intent to court video editors, 3D artists, and on-device AI developers who previously had to step up to higher SKU tiers or aftermarket upgrades just to obtain usable working memory. Pricing, exact launch dates, and regional availability for the RTX Spark variant have not yet been disclosed by ASUS or NVIDIA.
