Apple is finalizing preparations for a major product event on September 9, where it is expected to unveil the iPhone 18 Pro, the iPhone 18 Pro Max, and a long-anticipated foldable iPhone model. The launch marks one of the most significant hardware announcements in the company's recent history, as Apple enters the foldable smartphone segment for the first time while simultaneously refreshing its flagship Pro series.

The iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max are expected to succeed the current Pro lineup, continuing Apple's pattern of annual premium-tier upgrades. While specific technical specifications remain closely guarded, key features and pricing details have begun emerging ahead of the official unveiling, suggesting Apple is building toward a tightly coordinated global launch strategy. The inclusion of a foldable variant alongside the Pro series signals Apple's intent to compete directly with established foldable-device makers that have dominated the form factor for several years.

The foldable iPhone represents a notable strategic shift for Apple, which has historically waited to enter new hardware categories until it believes the technology and market readiness are sufficiently mature. The decision to bundle the foldable model with the Pro series launch rather than staging a separate event underscores the company's confidence in the device's readiness and its desire to position folding as a premium-tier feature rather than a niche experiment.

Pricing details, while still emerging, will be closely watched by analysts and consumers alike, particularly given the premium positioning of both the Pro Max and foldable variants. Apple's pricing strategy for the foldable model could set a new ceiling for iPhone costs, a metric that carries implications across the broader smartphone market and supply chain. The September 9 event is expected to provide full specifications, availability dates, and regional pricing.

The launch comes at a critical juncture for the global smartphone industry, which has been searching for compelling new form factors to reignite consumer upgrade cycles. Apple's entry into foldables, combined with its Pro-series refresh, could reshape competitive dynamics across premium segments, particularly in markets like India where Apple has been aggressively expanding its manufacturing footprint and retail presence.
