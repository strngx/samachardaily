Police departments across Georgia are urging parents to monitor their children's online activity following the rapid spread of a viral artificial intelligence trend themed around Dr. Seuss’s famous character, the Cat in the Hat. Local authorities issued advisories after observing an uptick in youth participation in the digital trend across various social media channels.

According to reports broadcast by WGAU Radio, law enforcement officials raised concerns over how young users are engaging with AI tools associated with the phenomenon. The warning highlights potential safety, privacy, and digital security risks that can arise when minors interact with unvetted artificial intelligence generators and online challenges.

While generative AI tools have gained massive popularity among teenagers for creating altered images, avatars, and interactive media, police caution that viral trends can sometimes expose children to data harvesting, inappropriate content, or online scams disguised as playful filters.

Parents are being advised to talk to their children about digital literacy, review privacy settings on smart devices, and supervise the download of third-party artificial intelligence applications. Officers emphasized that proactive parental guidance remains the primary defense against potential online risks associated with trending digital phenomena.

Law enforcement agencies continue to monitor the spread of the trend across regional online networks, advising families to report any suspicious activities or compromised accounts to local police authorities or school officials.
