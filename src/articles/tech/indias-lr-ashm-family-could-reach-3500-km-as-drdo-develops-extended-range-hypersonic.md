India’s Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) is advancing conceptual and engineering development for an extended-range variant of the Long-Range Anti-Ship Missile (LR-AShM) family, with defense analysts projecting potential strike envelopes reaching up to 3,500 kilometers, according to reporting by Indian Defence News.

The specialized missile platform is engineered to address emerging naval security requirements across the Indian Ocean Region (IOR), designed to counter hostile aircraft carrier strike groups and surface combatants at strategic standoff distances. The missile system incorporates advanced solid-fuel rocket boosters, scramjet propulsion modules, and terminal hypersonic glide trajectories to defeat modern shipborne air defense interception systems.

Naval defense strategists note that an anti-ship weapon with a 3,500-kilometer operational radius significantly strengthens India’s coastal and maritime anti-access/area-denial (A2/AD) architecture. Integrated with Indian Navy maritime patrol aircraft such as the Boeing P-8I Neptune and specialized naval surveillance satellites, the missile system enables rapid response capabilities across critical maritime choke points, including the Strait of Malacca and the Mozambique Channel.

The extended-range missile project represents a core pillar of India’s indigenous defense technological roadmap, reinforcing deterrence while reducing dependence on foreign missile technology for long-range naval power projection.
