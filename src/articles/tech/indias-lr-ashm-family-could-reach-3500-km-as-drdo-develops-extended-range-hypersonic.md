In January 2026, DRDO publicly displayed the LR‑AShM (Long‑Range Air‑to‑Surface Hypersonic Missile) during the Republic Day Parade, confirming that the core hypersonic propulsion and guidance systems have been validated in flight trials. The display marked the first visual confirmation of India’s indigenous long‑range hypersonic capability.

A senior DRDO scientist, cited in the agency’s own January 2026 newspaper‑clipping compilation, said development work is under way on extended‑range versions that could strike targets up to 3,500 km away. Engineers are focusing on boosting fuel efficiency, heat‑shield materials and advanced inertial navigation to sustain hypersonic speeds over the longer trajectory.

If realised, the 3,500 km LR‑AShM would place India among a handful of nations fielding operational hypersonic weapons with inter‑continental reach, potentially reshaping regional deterrence calculations and opening a new market for export‑oriented defence contracts.
