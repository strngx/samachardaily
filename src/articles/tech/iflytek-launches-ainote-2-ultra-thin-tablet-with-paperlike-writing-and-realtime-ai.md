Chinese AI specialist iFLYTEK announced the AINOTE 2, positioning it as an ultra‑thin tablet that merges the tactile feel of paper with on‑device, real‑time artificial intelligence. The company describes the device as a single‑piece solution where users can write, sketch, and interact with AI‑driven features without the bulk of conventional laptops or the distraction of separate smart pens.

iFLYTEK, best known for its speech‑recognition and natural‑language‑processing technologies, has been expanding its hardware portfolio to capitalize on the growing demand for AI‑enhanced productivity tools. The AINOTE 2 follows earlier attempts by the firm to embed AI into consumer electronics, leveraging its proprietary algorithms to offer instant transcription, language translation, and contextual suggestions as users write.

The tablet’s design emphasizes a paper‑like surface that mimics the friction and responsiveness of real paper, a feature that industry analysts say could appeal to students, educators, and professionals who prefer handwritten notes but desire immediate digital benefits. Real‑time AI integration means that as a user writes, the system can suggest corrections, summarize content, or even generate related research material, all without requiring an internet connection.

While detailed specifications such as screen size, processor type, or battery life have not been disclosed publicly, iFLYTEK’s marketing material highlights the device’s ultra‑thin profile, suggesting a focus on portability and ergonomics. The company also hints at a seamless ecosystem where the tablet syncs with its existing cloud services, allowing users to store and retrieve AI‑processed documents across devices.

The launch of the AINOTE 2 arrives at a time when the Indian and global markets are witnessing a surge in hybrid learning tools and remote‑work solutions. By combining a familiar writing experience with AI capabilities, iFLYTEK aims to capture a segment of users who are looking for a bridge between analog habits and digital efficiency, potentially reshaping how note‑taking is approached in both academic and corporate settings.
