A senior researcher at Anthropic, the artificial‑intelligence startup known for its safety‑first approach, announced his resignation on Monday, citing deep concerns about the trajectory of the broader AI industry.

In a brief statement to the media, the researcher warned that “the entire artificial intelligence industry is designing tools that humans will soon lose control of,” highlighting a perceived shift away from rigorous safety protocols toward unchecked capability scaling.

Anthropic, founded in 2020 by former OpenAI executives, has positioned itself as a counterweight to more aggressive AI development models, emphasizing alignment research and transparent governance. The departure of a key safety‑focused employee raises questions about internal disagreements on the company’s strategic direction.

Industry analysts note that the warning comes amid a wave of high‑profile AI releases, including large language models from major tech firms that are being rolled out with limited external oversight. Critics argue that rapid deployment outpaces the development of robust safety frameworks, potentially exposing users to unintended consequences.

The researcher’s resignation has sparked debate within the AI community, with some peers calling for a renewed focus on alignment research, while others contend that market pressures are driving a “race to the bottom” in safety standards.
