The latest installment of Brad Frost’s ‘Waking Up Excited’ podcast featured a candid conversation with Andy Clarke, a veteran of the digital design world known for his work at the BBC and later at Google. Frost, celebrated for popularising the Atomic Design methodology, used his platform to explore the personal side of a figure whose career has spanned the evolution of web design. The episode, titled “The ridiculous goo between my ears,” set a relaxed tone, allowing Clarke to discuss topics that rarely surface in typical tech interviews.

Clarke spoke at length about the pull of nostalgia, describing how objects from his childhood continue to shape his creative instincts. He highlighted the role of family as a grounding force, noting that conversations with his relatives often surface ideas that later influence his design thinking. Mental health emerged as a central theme; Clarke emphasized the importance of acknowledging stress and burnout in a high‑pressure industry, and he shared practical steps he takes to maintain balance, such as regular reflection and setting boundaries around work hours. Turning sixty, a milestone he is approaching, prompted him to contemplate legacy and the next chapter of his career, suggesting that experience can become a strategic asset rather than a hindrance.

The discussion resonated with listeners across the design community, many of whom see Clarke’s reflections as a mirror of their own journeys. By intertwining personal anecdotes with broader industry insights, the episode underscored how the human side of design—memory, wellbeing, and ageing—continues to influence the evolution of digital products.
