The social‑media platform Instagram announced today that it will retire the “AI creator” label that has been attached to synthetic personas and replace it with a more explicit “AI‑generated profile” tag. The change is aimed at making it easier for users to spot accounts that are run by artificial intelligence rather than a real person.

The previous label, introduced last year, was intended to give creators a way to signal that their content was produced with the help of generative AI tools. However, critics argued that the wording was ambiguous and that many AI‑driven accounts continued to operate without clear disclosure. Instagram’s new tag is designed to close that gap by attaching a visible marker directly to the profile name, ensuring that the AI nature of the account is front‑and‑center for anyone who visits the page.

Under the updated policy, any profile that carries the “AI‑generated profile” tag but does not clearly disclose its AI identity will face reduced recommendation reach. This means the algorithm will limit the account’s exposure in the Explore feed, suggested follows, and other discovery mechanisms. Instagram said the measure is a “reach penalty” intended to discourage covert AI activity while still allowing legitimate AI‑assisted creators to operate transparently.

The move has drawn mixed reactions from the creator community. Some AI‑focused influencers welcome the clarity, noting that it could build trust with followers who are increasingly wary of synthetic content. Others worry that the reach penalty could disproportionately affect smaller creators who rely on algorithmic discovery to grow their audiences, especially if they inadvertently miss the disclosure requirement.

Instagram’s decision comes amid a broader industry push to label AI‑generated media, with regulators and platforms worldwide grappling with how to balance innovation and misinformation. By tightening its labeling rules and tying them to algorithmic visibility, Instagram signals a more aggressive stance on transparency, echoing similar steps taken by competitors to flag deepfakes, synthetic voices, and other AI‑enhanced outputs.
