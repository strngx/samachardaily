Krafton, the South Korean studio best known for the global hit PUBG, disclosed a fresh $250 million infusion aimed at expanding its operations in India. The capital will be deployed to build capabilities outside its core gaming business, with a clear emphasis on artificial intelligence, robotics and other deep‑technology domains.

India has emerged as a pivotal market for Krafton over the past few years, driven largely by the massive popularity of PUBG Mobile, which consistently ranked among the country’s top‑downloaded games. The company’s existing user base and local partnerships have given it a foothold that it now seeks to leverage for broader technology initiatives.

The decision to diversify reflects Krafton’s strategic view that India’s burgeoning tech ecosystem offers fertile ground for AI research, robotics development and advanced engineering. Government incentives, a large pool of engineering talent and a fast‑growing startup landscape are cited as key factors encouraging the firm to allocate such a sizable sum beyond its traditional gaming revenue streams.

Industry analysts expect the investment to translate into new research centres, joint ventures with Indian tech firms and a wave of hiring for engineers, data scientists and robotics specialists. While exact locations have not been disclosed, the focus is likely to be on established tech hubs such as Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune, where ecosystems for deep‑tech innovation are already thriving.

Krafton’s move aligns with a broader trend of global gaming companies branching into adjacent technology fields, seeking to hedge against market saturation and to tap into high‑growth sectors. By committing $250 million, Krafton signals its confidence that India can serve as a launchpad for next‑generation AI and robotics products that may eventually complement its gaming portfolio.
