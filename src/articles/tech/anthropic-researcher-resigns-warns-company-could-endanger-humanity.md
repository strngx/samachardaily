Anthropic, the San Francisco‑based artificial‑intelligence startup that markets itself as a "more responsible" AI lab, faced an internal crisis on Monday when a senior researcher submitted a resignation letter citing existential danger. The researcher, who asked to remain anonymous for safety reasons, warned that the company’s current trajectory could lead to “the most aggressive of these scenarios” where AI systems become uncontrollable by the close of next year.

The resignation letter, obtained by Futurism, details the scientist’s growing alarm over Anthropic’s rapid scaling of large language models and its push to integrate them into high‑stakes applications without sufficient safety guardrails. The researcher argues that the lab’s internal risk assessments have been downplayed, and that pressure from investors to accelerate product roll‑outs has eclipsed the organization’s original ethical commitments.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI executives with the promise of building AI that aligns with human values and mitigates catastrophic outcomes. The company has attracted billions in venture funding and positioned itself as a counterweight to more aggressive competitors. However, insiders say internal debates over model size, training data, and deployment timelines have intensified, with some engineers expressing concern that the organization is edging toward the very risks it pledged to avoid.

Industry analysts note that Anthropic’s recent announcements—such as the launch of a new multimodal model and plans to commercialize AI assistants for enterprise—signal a shift toward faster market penetration. The departing researcher’s warning underscores a broader tension in the AI sector between rapid innovation and the need for robust safety protocols, a debate that has intensified after high‑profile incidents involving other AI firms.

The resignation has sparked reactions from investors and regulators alike. Venture capital partners have requested a detailed safety audit, while the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has signaled interest in reviewing Anthropic’s compliance with emerging AI governance frameworks. The company has not yet issued a public response beyond a brief statement affirming its commitment to responsible AI development.
