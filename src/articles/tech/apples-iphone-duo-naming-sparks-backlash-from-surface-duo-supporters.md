Apple announced its latest folding smartphone, the iPhone Duo, at a product launch that also introduced several other devices. The new model joins the iPhone 15 series and features a dual‑screen design that folds inward, positioning it as a direct competitor to other foldables on the market.

The naming choice has ignited a firestorm among fans of Microsoft’s Surface Duo, a line of dual‑screen Android devices that debuted in 2020. Critics argue that Apple’s use of the word “Duo” mirrors Microsoft’s branding and could cause confusion among consumers familiar with the Surface Duo ecosystem.

Industry observers note that Apple has a history of naming products with generic descriptors—such as “Pro,” “Max,” and “Mini”—but the overlap with a specific Microsoft trademark is unusual. Microsoft has not yet issued an official statement, but legal analysts suggest the company could explore trademark infringement claims if it believes the similarity dilutes its brand.

The iPhone Duo is priced competitively within the premium foldable segment, though exact figures for the Indian market have not been disclosed. Early reviewers anticipate that Apple’s entry will push other manufacturers to accelerate innovation and potentially lower prices for dual‑screen devices.

Consumers and tech commentators are watching closely to see whether the naming dispute will lead to a legal showdown or simply fade as both companies continue to roll out new hardware. For now, the controversy underscores the intense competition in the emerging foldable phone market.
