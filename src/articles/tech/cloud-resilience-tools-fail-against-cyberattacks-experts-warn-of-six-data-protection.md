A September 2026 Microsoft incident showed attackers using compromised Azure identities to delete cloud resources and target recovery protections in seven minutes, attempting over 100 storage account deletions. Cloud resilience tools designed for availability can be weaponized when an attacker holds sufficient privileges.

Six misconceptions leave teams exposed: S3 versioning is not a backup; multi-region replication may copy corrupted data; snapshots can be deleted if attackers reach the production account; infrastructure as code rebuilds environments but not customer data; stateless applications still write to databases; and audit logs critical for investigations are often omitted from recovery plans.

Experts urge verifying two points: whether stolen production credentials could delete recovery copies, and whether restorations have been tested. Executives will demand timelines, yet teams often cannot assess damage or safety of restored systems without prior validation.
