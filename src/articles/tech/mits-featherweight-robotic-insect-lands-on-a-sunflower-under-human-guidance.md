A flapping-wing microrobot built at MIT has landed on a sunflower under human guidance, with the featherweight craft weighing less than a paperclip. The demonstration places the machine among the smallest flying robots reported to complete a guided landing on a natural surface.

Unlike tethered or externally tracked micro-air vehicles, the robot carries its own sensors and computers, a configuration described as enabling free flight. That onboard payload is central to the design, since insect-scale aircraft have little margin for added mass.

Coverage of the work also points to crop pollination as a possible long-term use for such robots, framed as a future prospect rather than a demonstrated capability.
