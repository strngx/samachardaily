Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has initiated high-level consultations with major global technology executives, including Apple Chief Executive Tim Cook, to build industry cooperation for proposed national legislation establishing a mandatory minimum age for social media access and stricter artificial intelligence safety guidelines, according to Telecomliveweb.

The diplomatic and regulatory outreach follows the Australian federal government’s announcement of plans to introduce world-first legislative age verification requirements designed to prevent children under 14 or 16 from accessing algorithm-driven social media platforms. The initiative aims to mitigate youth mental health risks, curb online cyberbullying, and restrict predatory content delivery systems.

During bilateral discussions with technology leaders, Albanese emphasized that digital platforms share civic responsibility with parents to safeguard younger users. Discussions focused on privacy-preserving age-assurance technologies, algorithmic recommender safety audits, and establishing enforceable industry standards regarding deepfakes and non-consensual AI-generated imagery.

While global technology providers expressed broad support for youth online safety objectives, industry groups have raised operational questions regarding third-party biometric verification, data privacy protections for adult users, and cross-platform technical compliance. The Australian parliament is expected to deliberate on the statutory draft following regional testing trials.
