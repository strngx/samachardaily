The U.S. Department of Justice announced on Tuesday that it is investigating Nvidia's licensing deal with Groq, a San Francisco‑based artificial‑intelligence accelerator startup. The probe, reported by The New York Times, focuses on whether the agreement may violate antitrust laws by restricting competition in the fast‑growing AI hardware market.

Nvidia, the world’s leading GPU maker, entered into a licensing partnership with Groq earlier this year to allow the startup access to Nvidia’s proprietary software stack and hardware designs. Groq, founded in 2016, markets a line of low‑latency AI processors that compete with Nvidia’s own offerings for edge and data‑center workloads.

The DOJ’s antitrust unit typically examines deals that could create barriers to entry, enable price‑fixing, or result in exclusive arrangements that disadvantage rivals. While the agency has not disclosed the specific legal theories it is pursuing, officials said the investigation will assess the competitive impact of the licensing terms and any potential market‑share implications.

Industry analysts note that Nvidia’s dominance in GPU‑based AI training and inference has prompted scrutiny from regulators worldwide. A similar investigation into Nvidia’s acquisition of Arm was halted in 2022 after facing antitrust challenges in multiple jurisdictions. The current probe could signal heightened regulatory attention as AI hardware becomes a strategic sector.

Both companies have declined to comment on the investigation. Nvidia’s stock closed flat on Wednesday, while Groq’s private valuation remains undisclosed. The outcome of the DOJ review could influence future licensing strategies across the AI chip ecosystem.
