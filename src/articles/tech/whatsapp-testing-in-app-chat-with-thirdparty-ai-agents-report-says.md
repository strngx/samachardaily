According to a recent report, WhatsApp is quietly testing a new capability that would allow its users to start conversations with AI agents created by external developers. The trial is said to be limited to a subset of accounts, with the AI agents appearing as regular contacts in the chat list. Users would be able to type queries or commands and receive responses generated by the third‑party AI, all without leaving the familiar WhatsApp interface.

WhatsApp, owned by Meta Platforms, has long positioned itself as a simple, encrypted messaging service used worldwide. In recent years, Meta has been integrating generative‑AI tools across its product suite, including AI‑enhanced photo filters on Instagram and AI‑driven assistance in its Messenger app. The move to open the platform to external AI agents follows a broader industry trend where messaging services become hubs for a range of digital services, from payments to content discovery.

Proponents argue that embedding third‑party AI agents could make WhatsApp a one‑stop shop for tasks such as travel planning, customer support, or personal productivity, potentially increasing user engagement and creating new revenue streams for both Meta and AI developers. Critics, however, warn that the feature raises privacy and security concerns, especially given WhatsApp’s end‑to‑end encryption model. Determining how data shared with external AI agents is handled, and ensuring that malicious bots cannot exploit the platform, will be key challenges for the rollout.

Industry observers note that the testing phase comes at a time when regulators in several jurisdictions are scrutinising the use of AI in consumer applications. If the feature moves beyond the trial, Meta may need to provide transparency about data flows, obtain user consent for AI interactions, and comply with emerging AI‑specific guidelines. The company has not disclosed a timeline for a broader launch, and it remains unclear whether the feature will be offered to all users or remain a premium add‑on.

For everyday users, the prospect of chatting with an AI inside WhatsApp could blur the line between personal messaging and automated assistance. While some may welcome the convenience of getting instant answers without switching apps, others may be wary of introducing algorithmic interlocutors into private conversations. The outcome of the test will likely shape how messaging platforms balance innovation with the trust that users place in encrypted communication.
