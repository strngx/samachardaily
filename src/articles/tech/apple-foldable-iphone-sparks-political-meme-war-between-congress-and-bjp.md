Apple introduced its first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo, at an event in India, pricing the device at ₹2,99,900. The launch marks Apple's entry into the foldable segment, a market long dominated by Samsung and other Android manufacturers. The company positioned the iPhone Duo as a premium offering, emphasizing its advanced hinge technology and improved durability compared to earlier prototypes. The device features a 7.8‑inch inner display and a 6.5‑inch outer screen, positioning it as a direct competitor to Samsung's Galaxy Z series.

The announcement quickly took a political turn as the two major Indian parties, the Congress and the BJP, used the phone's high price as ammunition in their ongoing rivalry. Social media posts from both parties featured memes contrasting the cost of the iPhone Duo with everyday expenses, accusing each other of being out of touch with middle‑class consumers. The exchange highlighted how technology launches can become proxies for broader political narratives. The memes went viral across Indian social platforms, prompting responses from technology journalists who noted the unusual political dimension of a product launch.

Industry analysts point out that the iPhone Duo is Apple's most Korea‑dependent iPhone to date, with key components such as the flexible display and hinge supplied by South Korean firms. This reliance underscores the intricate supply chain linking Apple to its Asian partners, and it may expose the company to geopolitical risks should tensions on the peninsula escalate. The dependence also reflects the broader trend of smartphone manufacturers integrating advanced materials from specialized suppliers. Additionally, the hinge mechanism is reportedly co‑developed with a Japanese firm, illustrating the multinational nature of the supply chain.

The ₹2,99,900 price tag has sparked a debate over the device's value, especially when compared to its expected United States cost. Critics argue that the Indian pricing includes steep import duties and taxes, creating a significant price gap that could limit adoption among price‑sensitive consumers. The sticker shock has prompted discussions about whether Apple will adjust its pricing strategy for emerging markets. Apple has not yet disclosed whether it will introduce a lower‑priced variant for the Indian market.

In response, Samsung took to social media to mock Apple's late entry into the foldable arena, posting a video that highlighted its own line of foldable phones available for years. The Korean giant's marketing push serves as a reminder of the competitive pressure Apple faces as it seeks to capture market share in a segment where Samsung has established a strong foothold. Observers expect the rivalry to intensify as both companies roll out next‑generation models. The company is expected to reveal more details about its foldable strategy during its annual product event later this year.
