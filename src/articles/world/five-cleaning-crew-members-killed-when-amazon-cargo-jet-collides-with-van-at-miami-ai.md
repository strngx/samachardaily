A tragic collision at Miami International Airport left five cleaning‑crew workers dead when an Amazon‑operated cargo jet slammed into the van they were using to transport equipment. The crew, employed by a third‑party contractor, had been wrapping up a demanding shift when the aircraft, a Boeing 767‑300F, taxied onto the runway and failed to stop in time.

The victims – identified as Yoel Rodriguez Naranjo, Carlos Mendez, Luis Alvarez, Ahmed Patel and Maria Gomez – were preparing to leave the airport grounds when the jet, bound for a domestic freight run, entered the taxiway at excessive speed. Airport surveillance footage shows the van positioned near the service road, a standard staging area for ground‑service personnel, moments before the impact.

Emergency responders arrived within minutes, but the force of the crash proved fatal for all five workers. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has opened an investigation into the incident, focusing on possible pilot error, runway lighting conditions, and communication protocols between the aircraft and ground‑control. Preliminary statements from the airline indicate the flight crew reported a malfunctioning brake system, which may have contributed to the inability to halt the aircraft.

Family members of the deceased have been notified, and a lawyer representing Yoel Rodriguez Naranjo has filed a wrongful‑death claim against Amazon and the airline operating the cargo jet. The filing alleges negligence in safety procedures and inadequate coordination with airport ground staff. The case could set a precedent for liability in similar ground‑air collisions at busy commercial hubs.

Miami International Airport has temporarily suspended cargo operations on the affected runway pending the FAA’s findings. Airport officials say they are reviewing all ground‑vehicle routing policies and will issue revised safety guidelines to prevent future tragedies. The incident has reignited calls from labor unions for stricter protections for contract cleaning crews who work in high‑risk airport environments.
