A federal wrongful‑death lawsuit was lodged on Tuesday in the Southern District of Florida, accusing Amazon.com Inc., charter carrier 21 Air, and several aircraft‑leasing entities of negligence following the fatal crash of a cargo jet at Miami International Airport on March 15.

The complaint alleges that Amazon, which contracts 21 Air to transport packages, failed to ensure the aircraft was properly maintained and that the leasing firms supplied a plane that did not meet safety standards. The suit seeks compensatory damages for the families of the three crew members who perished and punitive damages for alleged corporate misconduct.

According to the filing, the Boeing 737‑800 freighter, operated by 21 Air under a lease from Global Aviation Holdings and AerCap, suffered a catastrophic engine failure during take‑off, striking the runway and igniting a fire that trapped the pilot, co‑pilot and flight engineer. All three were pronounced dead at the scene.

Amazon’s legal team has not yet responded to requests for comment. 21 Air, which operates a fleet of cargo aircraft for e‑commerce firms, issued a brief statement expressing “deep sympathy” for the victims but declined to discuss the lawsuit. The leasing companies listed in the complaint include AerCap, Global Aviation Holdings and a lesser‑known entity, SkyLease Corp.

Legal experts note that suing a logistics giant like Amazon for an aircraft accident is unusual but not unprecedented, pointing to prior cases where retailers were held liable for third‑party carrier negligence when they exercised significant control over transport operations.
