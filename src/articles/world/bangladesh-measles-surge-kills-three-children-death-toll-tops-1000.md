Bangladesh's health authorities confirmed that three more children succumbed to measles on Wednesday, pushing the outbreak's death toll to 1,002 since the start of the year, according to the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS). The fatalities were recorded in the 24‑hour period ending Wednesday morning, marking the latest spike in a crisis that officials describe as unprecedented.

The measles outbreak, which began earlier this year, has strained Bangladesh's public‑health system, already grappling with routine immunisation gaps and seasonal disease burdens. Health officials say vaccination coverage in several rural districts fell below the World Health Organization's target, creating pockets of susceptibility that the virus has exploited.

DGHS spokesperson Dr. Farhana Islam warned that the situation remains volatile, citing limited supplies of measles‑containing vaccine (MCV) and a shortage of trained health workers to conduct rapid outreach campaigns. "We are scaling up emergency immunisation drives, but logistical challenges persist," she said in a briefing on Thursday.

International partners, including UNICEF and the WHO, have pledged additional vaccine doses and technical support. However, the agencies stress that community engagement is crucial; misinformation about vaccine safety continues to hamper uptake in some areas. Local NGOs are mobilising volunteers to disseminate accurate information and assist with door‑to‑door vaccination efforts.

The government has declared the measles surge a public health emergency, authorising the deployment of mobile clinics to hard‑to‑reach regions and allocating emergency funds for cold‑chain equipment. Analysts note that without a swift, coordinated response, the death toll could climb further, reversing years of progress in child health indicators.
