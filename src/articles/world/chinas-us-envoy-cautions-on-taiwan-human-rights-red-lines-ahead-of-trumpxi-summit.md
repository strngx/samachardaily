China’s Ambassador to the United States has delivered a firm diplomatic address in Washington, outlining Beijing’s fundamental sovereign "red lines" regarding Taiwan and domestic human rights ahead of high-level bilateral summits between Chinese President Xi Jinping and American political leadership, according to reporting by U.S. News & World Report.

Speaking before international policy scholars and business leaders, the ambassador emphasized that the Taiwan question remains the bedrock political foundation and the foremost sensitive issue in China-U.S. relations. The envoy cautioned that foreign arms sales and diplomatic official exchanges with Taipei undermine regional cross-strait stability and risk severe diplomatic escalation between the world's two largest economies.

The ambassador also rejected American critiques regarding domestic governance in Xinjiang, Hong Kong, and Tibet, asserting that sovereign internal affairs cannot be utilized as bargaining chips in bilateral trade or geopolitical negotiations. However, the envoy noted that Beijing remains committed to maintaining open military-to-military communication channels, cooperating on global climate finance, and stabilizing bilateral commercial trade flows.

Foreign policy analysts observe that the diplomatic posturing reflects both nations’ efforts to establish firm negotiating boundaries ahead of crucial trade and economic deliberations, seeking to manage strategic competition while preventing unintended military confrontations in the Indo-Pacific.
