WASHINGTON — United States Vice President JD Vance has declared that Iran’s long-standing strategic hold over the Strait of Hormuz is now "effectively gone," pointing to a progressive erosion of Tehran’s military posture in the region. Speaking on the broader military confrontational dynamics involving the US, Israel, and Iran, Vance urged an immediate end to ongoing attacks against commercial shipping vessels navigating the critical international waterway.

The Vice President asserted that Tehran’s operational capacities in and around the vital water passage are diminishing on a daily basis. According to Vance, the reduction in Iranian maritime leverage means that attempts to exert dominance over the choke point are "virtually over." The statement reflects Washington's assessment that recent containment measures and regional military actions have severely constrained Iran's ability to project power along its coastal maritime perimeter.

The Strait of Hormuz remains a vital artery for global trade, through which a major portion of the world's sea-borne crude oil and liquefied natural gas passes daily. Attacks on commercial shipping and naval disruptions along this route have historically sent shockwaves through international energy markets and heightened security risks for global merchant fleets. Vance’s declaration directly targets Tehran's historic tactic of leveraging control over the strait during periods of escalating geopolitical friction.

Amid escalating regional hostilities among US, Israeli, and Iranian forces, Vance reiterated the imperative of maintaining freedom of navigation in international waters. The Vice President urged all hostile forces to halt strikes on commercial shipping, emphasizing that Washington and its allies remain focused on neutralizing security threats to global commerce and ensuring that critical maritime routes remain open.

The Vice President’s stance signals an assertive effort by the administration to project military confidence and stabilize commercial shipping corridors. By publicly declaring Iran’s operational grip on the Strait of Hormuz broken, the US leadership aims to deter further maritime harassment while reinforcing security guarantees to international trading partners and regional allies operating in the Persian Gulf.
