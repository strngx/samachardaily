Veteran conservative pundit Pete Hegseth reignited controversy on Thursday after posting a photo of Canadian military cadets on his X account with a caption that sexualised their uniforms. The post, which referenced the cadets’ attire as “sexy,” was quickly condemned by a broad cross‑section of Canadian public figures, including former central‑bank governor Mark Carney, who warned that such mockery undermines the credibility of Canada’s armed forces.

Hegseth, a former Marine and regular commentator on Fox News, has a history of provocative posts targeting political opponents. In this instance, he framed the cadets—young men and women enrolled in the Canadian Armed Forces’ youth program—as objects of ridicule, prompting a backlash that spilled over into Canada’s major newspapers. The Toronto Star published a letter to the editor describing Hegseth’s remarks as “shameful words” that eclipsed even the most contentious actions of the Trump administration, while the Globe and Mail ran a piece highlighting a Canadian woman soldier’s anger at the post.

Actor and activist George Clooney entered the fray, using his social‑media platform to call out Hegseth for “mocking the dedication of Canada’s youth.” Clooney’s intervention amplified the story, drawing international attention to what many Canadians view as a disrespectful attack on their military tradition. CBC reported that Canada’s top soldier publicly affirmed pride in the cadet program, rejecting Hegseth’s characterization and emphasizing the program’s role in fostering leadership and civic duty.

The controversy also sparked a broader conversation about the treatment of military uniforms in public discourse. Critics argue that Hegseth’s post perpetuates a gendered narrative that reduces service members to aesthetic objects, a concern echoed in multiple letters to the editor across the country. The letters, published on September 4, collectively demanded accountability from Hegseth and called for social‑media platforms to enforce stricter standards on harassment and hate speech.

In response to the mounting criticism, Hegseth has not issued a formal apology, instead defending his post as “satire.” However, the sustained pressure from Canadian officials, celebrities, and ordinary citizens suggests that the episode may have lasting repercussions for his public profile and for the broader debate on respectful discourse surrounding the armed forces.
