Ljubljana saw a massive street demonstration on Thursday as citizens converged to denounce the Slovenian government's decision to establish an embassy for Israel, a diplomatic step announced by Prime Minister Janez Janša. The protest, organized by a coalition of left‑wing parties, human‑rights groups and civic NGOs, filled the city centre, with chants demanding a reversal of the policy and calling for Janša's removal from office.

Prime Minister Janša, who leads a nationalist coalition, has framed the embassy opening as a strategic realignment aimed at strengthening economic and security ties with Israel. His announcement follows months of diplomatic friction with the European Union over rule‑of‑law concerns, and comes as his government faces increasing domestic criticism for perceived authoritarian tendencies.

Opposition lawmakers seized on the decision to file an impeachment motion, alleging that Janša acted unconstitutionally by resetting foreign policy without parliamentary approval. The motion, submitted to the National Assembly, cites the embassy opening as evidence of Janša's disregard for democratic norms and the country's longstanding tradition of neutrality in Middle‑East affairs.

The protestors, many carrying Palestinian flags and signs reading “Stop the war” and “Defend democracy,” marched from Prešeren Square to the government building, where they staged a sit‑in. Police reported a peaceful demonstration, though several arrests were made for blocking traffic. International observers noted the event as part of a broader wave of public dissent across Europe over Middle‑East policies.

Analysts warn that the political fallout could destabilise Slovenia’s coalition government, potentially prompting early elections or a reshuffle of ministerial posts. The impeachment debate is expected to dominate parliamentary sessions in the coming weeks, while diplomatic channels remain open as Israel prepares to send its first ambassador to Ljubljana later this year.
