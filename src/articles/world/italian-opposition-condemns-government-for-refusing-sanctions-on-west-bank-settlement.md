On Wednesday, Italy’s main opposition parties publicly rebuked the coalition government for declining to join a multilateral initiative that imposes trade restrictions on Israeli settlements deemed illegal under international law. The criticism was reported by La Repubblica and echoed by Anadolu, highlighting a growing rift over foreign‑policy alignment with European partners.

Five Star Movement leader Giuseppe Conte, former prime minister, urged the cabinet to "show a shred of dignity" and support an opposition‑drafted bill that would align Italy with the sanctions coalition. Conte argued that the failure to act undermines Italy’s credibility on human‑rights issues and signals a double standard in its diplomatic posture.

Democratic Party head Elly Schlein echoed the sentiment, labeling the government's abstention as "shameful" and a missed opportunity to defend international law. Schlein warned that Italy’s silence could erode its standing within the European Union, where several member states have already adopted measures restricting imports from the settlements.

The proposed sanctions target goods produced in the occupied West Bank, aiming to pressure Israel to halt settlement expansion. While countries such as the United Kingdom, France and the Netherlands have signed on, Rome has so far cited economic concerns and the need for a coordinated EU approach as reasons for its non‑participation.

Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani has not yet responded to the opposition’s accusations, but the debate is set to intensify in parliament as the opposition bill moves toward a vote. The outcome could determine whether Italy aligns with the broader European stance or continues a more cautious diplomatic line.
