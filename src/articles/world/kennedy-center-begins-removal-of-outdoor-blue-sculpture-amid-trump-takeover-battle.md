Workers at the Kennedy Center have begun removing the large outdoor sculpture known as 'Blue' from the performing arts complex's grounds, a move that marks the latest flashpoint in the protracted battle over the institution's direction. The decision to dismantle the artwork, which has long been a familiar feature of the center's landscaped terraces overlooking the Potomac River in Washington, D.C., comes amid a wider reshaping of the venue following the Trump administration's intervention in its governance.

The sculpture had become a focal point for critics who argued it clashed with the aesthetic and cultural vision now being pursued by the center's new leadership. According to multiple outlets tracking the development, crews were seen tearing down the piece as the institutional fight over the Kennedy Center's programming continued to intensify. Coverage from CNN, The New York Times, The Hill, USA Today, and The Independent has all highlighted the removal as a visible symbol of the broader upheaval at one of the nation's premier cultural institutions.

The dispute traces back to the Trump administration's effort to assert greater control over the Kennedy Center, including changes to its board and programming priorities. The removal of 'Blue' follows a series of contested decisions that have drawn attention from arts advocates, lawmakers, and the broader cultural community. While the Kennedy Center has historically been viewed as a bipartisan showcase for the performing arts, recent months have seen it emerge as a test case for how federal influence can reshape cultural institutions.

Named details about the sculpture's artist, the specific cost of removal, and any official statement from the Kennedy Center's leadership were not available in the source material. What is clear from the coverage is that the physical dismantling of the artwork represents more than a maintenance decision — it is being read as a symbolic act tied to the ongoing power struggle over the institution's future identity and direction.
