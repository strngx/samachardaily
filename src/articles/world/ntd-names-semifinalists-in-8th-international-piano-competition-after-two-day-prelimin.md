New Tang Dynasty Television (NTD) has announced the semifinalists of its 8th International Piano Competition, wrapping up a preliminary round that spanned two days of performances. The contest, organized by the U.S.-based media outlet linked to the Falun Gong movement, is one of several classical music events the network has hosted as part of its broader cultural programming.

The preliminary stage drew competitors from across the international piano community, each contending for a place in the semifinals. NTD reporter Nikou Bani delivered the announcement of the advancing pianists, marking the transition from the opening rounds to the next stage of the competition. Specific names of the semifinalists and details about their performances were not included in the source dispatch.

NTD's International Piano Competition is held periodically and is designed to showcase classical artistry aligned with the network's traditional aesthetic preferences. Past editions have drawn young pianists competing for prize money and concert opportunities, and the competition has become a fixture on NTD's annual events calendar alongside its broader festival programming. The competition also serves as a platform for performers who specialize in traditional repertoire.

With the semifinalists now identified, the competition will move into its next phase, though the venue, exact dates, and further details of the upcoming rounds were not specified in the source material. The semifinal performances are expected to determine which pianists advance to the final stage of the contest. NTD has not yet publicly released additional logistical information about the remainder of the competition schedule.

Founded in 2001 and headquartered in New York, NTD operates a constellation of media outlets and organizes cultural events that include international music competitions, dance showcases, and traditional Chinese arts festivals. The piano competition is among its longest-running cultural programs, reflecting the network's stated mission to promote classical traditions through global broadcasting.
