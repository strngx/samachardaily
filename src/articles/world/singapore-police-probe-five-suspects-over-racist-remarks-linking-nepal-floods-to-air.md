Singapore police announced on Tuesday that they are investigating five people for allegedly posting racist comments online that linked the devastating floods in Nepal to the national carrier Air India. The police statement said the remarks were deemed hateful and potentially incite communal tension, prompting a formal inquiry under the country's Protection from Online Falsehoods and Manipulation Act.

The floods in Nepal, which began in early August, have caused widespread damage across the Kathmandu valley and the Terai region, displacing thousands and prompting an international humanitarian response. Air India has been among the airlines offering chartered flights to transport relief supplies and aid workers into the affected areas, a fact that appears to have been twisted in the online posts.

Authorities said the five suspects, whose identities have not been disclosed, used social media platforms to circulate the comments, which suggested that Air India was somehow responsible for the disaster. Police are reviewing the digital footprints of the accounts involved and have issued warnings that similar content could attract criminal charges for hate speech and misinformation.

Singapore has a track record of strict enforcement against online hate, especially when it involves foreign nationals or cross‑border incidents. The investigation follows earlier cases where individuals were prosecuted for spreading communal or racial animus online, underscoring the city‑state's zero‑tolerance stance on digital hate speech.

Legal experts note that the probe could lead to fines, imprisonment, or both, depending on the severity of the offenses and whether the suspects are found to have intended to incite hostility. The police have urged the public to report any further hateful content related to the Nepal floods or any other disaster.
