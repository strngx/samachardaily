Former Health and Human Services senior adviser Calley Means publicly demanded Saturday that White House chief of staff Susie Wiles and Trump political adviser James Blair stop what he called harassing texts and calls following his appearance on Joe Rogan's podcast. Means challenged them to identify any factual errors in writing and dared them to pursue a lawsuit, which he said would trigger discovery into whether Ballard Partners and Continental Strategy exerted undue influence on White House policy.

Blair, a Wiles ally who recently left the White House to lead the GOP's midterm effort, denied harassment within the hour. He said he had called Means the previous night, received a message in return, and warned of a defamation suit before ending the exchange. Blair dismissed Means's complaints as a "poor me act" and said he had "not even gotten started."

During the Rogan interview, Means alleged Wiles allowed midterm fundraising to drive health policy and that firms hiring her daughter, a Continental Strategy partner, received preferential treatment. He also accused Blair of accepting sugar industry money. Hours after the interview circulated, Trump announced Stephen Miller would replace Wiles on Monday. Means departed HHS in October, citing frustration over stalled "Make America Healthy Again" initiatives.
