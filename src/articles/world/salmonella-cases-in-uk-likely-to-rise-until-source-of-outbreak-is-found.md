Public health authorities in the United Kingdom have confirmed a growing outbreak of salmonella infections that appears to be tied to imported eggs used by a number of cafés and restaurants. The first cases were identified in early August, and since then the number of reported infections has risen steadily, prompting the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) to launch a coordinated investigation. At present, dozens of individuals have been diagnosed with salmonella gastroenteritis, and one patient, a 68‑year‑old man, succumbed to the infection after developing severe complications. 

Epidemiologists are focusing on the supply chain for eggs that are imported from overseas producers and then distributed to food‑service outlets across several English regions. Preliminary traceback work suggests that the eggs may have been sourced from a single exporter, but the exact farm or processing facility has not yet been identified. In the meantime, UKHSA has issued advisories urging cafés, restaurants and other food‑service businesses to halt the use of the implicated eggs, to implement stricter temperature controls, and to ensure thorough cooking of egg‑based dishes. The agency also recommends that anyone who has recently eaten eggs at a café or restaurant and is experiencing symptoms such as vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal cramps or fever seek medical attention promptly. 

The outbreak has sparked concern among consumer groups and prompted the Food Standards Agency (FSA) to increase inspections of imported egg consignments. While the investigation continues, officials stress that the risk to the wider public remains limited as long as the eggs are cooked properly, but they warn that the situation could deteriorate if the source remains undiscovered and contaminated eggs continue to circulate in the food‑service sector.
