British editorial broadcaster GB News broke its regular schedule on Thursday to air a live, hour‑long interview with former U.S. President Donald Trump. The interview, billed as a "tell‑all" session, was the network's most high‑profile political booking in recent months and marked the first time the London‑based outlet has given Trump a dedicated platform.

The decision to pre‑empt regular news and commentary slots underscored the interview's perceived news value. GB News, which positions itself as a right‑leaning alternative to the UK public‑service broadcasters, promoted the segment heavily on its website and social channels, promising viewers an unfiltered look at Trump's post‑presidential agenda.

Audience reaction was swift and largely negative. Social‑media users and viewer comment boards repeatedly quoted a line that trended across platforms: "I've seen healthier‑looking corpses." The phrase, used to mock Trump's appearance and perceived vitality, dominated the online discourse and eclipsed discussion of the interview's substantive content.

While the full transcript of the interview remains unavailable to the public, the backlash suggests that Trump's remarks—whether about his health, political ambitions, or personal anecdotes—failed to resonate positively with the British audience. Media analysts noted that the episode illustrates the challenges U.S. political figures face when courting foreign broadcasters, where cultural expectations and humor can clash with the interviewee's style.

GB News has a history of courting controversial figures to boost ratings, and the Trump interview fits that pattern. The network's owner, Sir Paul Marshall, has previously defended the outlet's editorial independence, arguing that giving a platform to high‑profile personalities, regardless of their polarising nature, serves the public interest. However, the viewer outcry may force the channel to reassess its editorial calculus for future high‑stakes interviews.
