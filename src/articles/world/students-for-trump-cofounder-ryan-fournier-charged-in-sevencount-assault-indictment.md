A federal grand jury has returned a seven‑count indictment against Ryan Fournier, who helped launch the campus‑based political organization Students for Trump. The indictment alleges that Fournier assaulted his girlfriend, with the charges ranging from simple assault to aggravated assault, as detailed in the filing.

Students for Trump, founded in 2016, positions itself as a network of college students supporting the policies of former President Donald Trump. Fournier has been a public face of the group, frequently appearing at rallies and on social media to promote its agenda. His role as co‑founder has made the legal case a focal point for both supporters and critics of the organization.

According to the indictment, prosecutors have identified seven separate counts tied to the alleged assault. While the filing does not disclose the exact dates of each alleged incident, it states that the accusations were made by Fournier’s girlfriend, who filed a complaint that led to the grand jury’s involvement. The charges carry potential penalties that could include imprisonment, fines, and a permanent criminal record if convicted.

The Students for Trump organization has not issued an official statement regarding the indictment, and attempts to reach its spokesperson for comment were unsuccessful. Legal analysts note that the case will likely proceed through the federal court system, where a preliminary hearing will determine whether the charges will move forward to trial.

The indictment arrives at a time when political student groups across the United States are under heightened scrutiny for alleged misconduct among their leaders. Fournier’s legal troubles could have ripple effects on the organization’s recruitment, fundraising, and public perception, especially as the group prepares for upcoming campus events.
