U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow on Friday for a new diplomatic push aimed at ending Russia's invasion of Ukraine, according to NPR. The visit marks the latest in a series of high-level contacts between Washington and the Kremlin as both sides explore whether a negotiated settlement is achievable more than four years into the full-scale war.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy moved to shape the agenda of the talks by publicly calling for a halt to air strikes during the envoys' stay in the Russian capital. The appeal was framed as a confidence-building gesture — a temporary cessation of long-range drone and missile attacks that have devastated Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure throughout the conflict. Zelenskyy's request put the ball squarely in Moscow's court, framing the diplomatic moment as a test of whether Russia is willing to demonstrate restraint at the negotiating table.

As of the visit, Moscow had not issued any public response to Zelenskyy's proposed pause. The silence underscored the persistent gap between the two sides' stated positions and highlighted the difficulty facing the U.S. intermediaries. Witkoff, a real estate developer and close associate of U.S. President Donald Trump, has emerged as a central channel for back-channel communications with the Kremlin. Kushner, Trump's son-in-law and former senior White House adviser, has accompanied him on previous diplomatic trips to the region, lending the mission both political weight and a direct line to the U.S. president.

The Moscow meeting comes against the backdrop of parallel developments in Ukraine, including continued fighting along the front lines and renewed debate inside Europe about the future of military support for Kyiv. European allies have watched the U.S.-led track warily, anxious that any settlement not fully involve them could reshape the security architecture of the continent. For Ukraine, the talks carry significant risk: any framework that emerges from Moscow would need to be weighed against the country's stated insistence on territorial integrity and long-term security guarantees.

The outcome of the Witkoff-Kushner mission remains uncertain. With no public Russian response to the air-strike pause and no scheduled joint statement from the talks, the trip is being treated by observers as an exploratory contact rather than a final negotiating round. What is clear is that the Trump administration continues to invest senior diplomatic capital in pursuing a deal, even as the underlying conflict grinds on and the humanitarian toll on Ukraine continues to mount.
