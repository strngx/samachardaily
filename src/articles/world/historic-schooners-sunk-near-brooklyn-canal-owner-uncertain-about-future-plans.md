Two historic schooners that once graced a Maine-themed waterfront district and were associated with Jacques Cousteau's resting place now lie at the bottom of New York City's Gowanus Canal. The vessel owners, operating under a single corporate entity, announced the tragic loss of both ships approximately two months prior, leaving the public and maritime historians alike to await clarification on their ultimate disposition.

The company responsible for the vessels has not yet made a definitive decision regarding what to do with the sunken schooners. While the initial sinking drew attention due to the unique historical significance of the ships—one having been featured in a Maine-themed neighborhood and the other linked to the legendary French oceanographer—the lack of action from management has sparked concern among stakeholders who hope for a resolution that honors the ships' legacy.

As of the latest update, the owner continues to weigh options ranging from potential salvage operations to museum preservation, though no formal plan has been publicly disclosed. The ambiguity surrounding the fate of these artifacts has generated mixed reactions within the local community and among maritime heritage advocates who believe the ships represent irreplaceable pieces of nautical history.

The incident occurred in the Gowanus Canal area of Brooklyn, a region already known for its industrial past and emerging cultural revival. With the two-month timeframe since the sinking, there is mounting pressure for the company to provide transparency and take meaningful steps toward resolving the situation, lest the vessels become permanent reminders of unaddressed stewardship failures.
