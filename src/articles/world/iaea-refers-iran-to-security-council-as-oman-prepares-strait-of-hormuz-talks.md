The Board of Governors of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) has formally approved a diplomatic resolution referring Iran’s nuclear program to the United Nations Security Council over non-compliance with safeguard inspections, occurring simultaneously as regional mediator Oman prepares to host emergency diplomatic consultations regarding maritime security in the Strait of Hormuz, according to reporting by Middle East Monitor.

The IAEA resolution, supported by Western powers, cited Tehran’s persistent refusal to grant international inspectors access to undeclared nuclear sites and its ongoing accumulation of uranium enriched up to 60% purity, a threshold closely approaching weapons-grade material. The referral initiates formal review procedures that could lead to the reimposition of multilateral UN economic sanctions.

In tandem with the nuclear standoff, the Sultanate of Oman is coordinating regional maritime security discussions in Muscat following recent tanker harassment incidents and escalating naval skirmishes in the vital Persian Gulf shipping corridor. The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately one-fifth of global petroleum liquid consumption, making maritime free navigation vital to international energy markets.

Middle Eastern geopolitical analysts observe that the concurrent diplomatic tracks highlight the delicate security balance in the region. Regional diplomats are striving to preserve dialogue channels to prevent naval flare-ups from escalating into broader regional military conflict.
