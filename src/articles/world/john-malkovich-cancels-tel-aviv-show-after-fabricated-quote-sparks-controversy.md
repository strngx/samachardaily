John Malkovich has withdrawn from a planned performance of his play “The Music Critic” in Tel Aviv, citing a false quote that promoters attributed to him as the reason for the cancellation. The quote, which suggested Malkovich was voicing support for a contentious political stance, was later identified as fabricated and not spoken by the actor.

The cancellation was announced by Malkovich’s representatives on Monday, stating that the misinformation had created a hostile environment that made proceeding with the show untenable. The actor’s team emphasized that the quote was never made by Malkovich and that the decision was taken to avoid further misrepresentation.

Promoters of the Tel Aviv event had reportedly circulated the spurious statement on social media and in press releases, prompting backlash from both supporters and critics of the alleged position. The false attribution quickly spread, leading to calls for boycotts and intense media scrutiny.

Malkovich, known for his eclectic film and theater work, has previously performed internationally without incident. This is the first instance of a scheduled appearance being withdrawn due to fabricated statements. The incident highlights the growing challenges artists face in navigating misinformation in the digital age.

The play, slated for a limited run in 2027, was set to be staged at the Habima Theatre, a venue that has hosted numerous international productions. With the cancellation, ticket holders have been advised to seek refunds, and the theater’s management expressed disappointment while reaffirming its commitment to artistic freedom.
