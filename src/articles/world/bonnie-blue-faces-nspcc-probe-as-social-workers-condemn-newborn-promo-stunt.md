British adult performer Bonnie Blue has been reported to the NSPCC after using her newborn baby in a promotional stunt deemed inappropriate by child welfare advocates and Conservative MPs. The controversy erupted when social workers raised concerns about a video featuring the infant, prompting formal complaints to the children's charity.

The Tories condemned the act as 'grotesque' in a coordinated response, with several MPs calling for immediate investigation into potential child safeguarding breaches. Social workers echoed these concerns, describing the footage as 'disturbing' and questioning the suitability of exposing a newborn to adult entertainment contexts.

Authorities are now reviewing whether any child protection protocols were violated. The NSPCC has acknowledged receiving multiple reports and is assessing the situation. Legal experts note that while no laws may have been broken, the incident raises serious questions about consent and child welfare in digital content creation.

The incident has reignited debates about influencer responsibilities and the boundaries of adult content promotion. Critics argue that using minors—even indirectly—in such material undermines protections meant to shield children from inappropriate exposure.

Bonnie Blue has not publicly responded to the allegations. Her representatives previously stated that all content was produced consensually and within legal guidelines, but declined further comment amid the ongoing scrutiny.
