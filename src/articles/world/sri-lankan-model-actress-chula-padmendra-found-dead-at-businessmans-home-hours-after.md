Sri Lankan model and actress Chula Padmendra Kumarapathirana has been found dead at the age of 46, just hours after attending the Universal Woman Sri Lanka 2026 beauty pageant. The discovery was made at the residence of a businessman, and authorities have opened an investigation to determine the circumstances surrounding her death, according to a Moneycontrol report. The timing of her death, coming immediately after a public appearance at a high-profile event, has heightened public and media attention in Colombo and beyond.

Chula Padmendra Kumarapathirana was a familiar figure in Sri Lankan entertainment, having built a career as both a model and an actress over a number of years. Her participation in Universal Woman Sri Lanka 2026 placed her among contestants at a pageant that has drawn attention as a platform for women in the country. The event's timing, on the night preceding her death, has become a focal point for investigators seeking to reconstruct her final hours and verify the sequence of events leading from the pageant venue to the businessman's home where she was found.

Sri Lankan police have moved into the early stages of an inquiry, working to establish how Padmendra came to be at the businessman's residence and what transpired there. Investigators are expected to examine forensic evidence, interview attendees from the pageant, and question the homeowner and any other individuals who may have been present. While no official cause of death has been disclosed in the publicly available reporting, the case is being treated as one requiring formal police scrutiny given the profile of the deceased and the circumstances of her being found at a private residence rather than her own home.

The news has prompted an outpouring of reaction within Sri Lanka's entertainment industry and among fans who followed Padmendra's work on screen and on the runway. Colleagues and admirers have taken to social media to express shock at the sudden loss of a 46-year-old performer still active in the public eye. Advocacy groups focused on women's safety have also cited the case as underscoring the need for swift and transparent investigations when female public figures die under unclear circumstances, particularly when private residences are involved.

As the probe unfolds, attention is likely to focus on the businessman's role and on the chain of custody of any witness statements taken in the immediate aftermath. Sri Lankan authorities have not yet indicated whether any arrests have been made or whether foul play has been formally identified as a factor. The post-mortem findings, expected to be released through official police channels, are likely to shape the trajectory of the investigation and the public narrative around Padmendra's death in the days ahead.
