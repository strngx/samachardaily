French President Emmanuel Macron directly challenged United States President Donald Trump's characterization of diplomatic progress in the Middle East during an address to the 81st United Nations General Assembly on September 22, 2026, decrying ongoing humanitarian restrictions in the Gaza Strip. The diplomatic exchange unfolded as both leaders delivered major foreign policy addresses from the UN rostrum in New York.

Earlier in the assembly session, President Trump portrayed his administration's 20-Point Gaza peace framework as a major diplomatic achievement, asserting that his diplomatic engagement had successfully brought stability to the region, secured living hostages, and ended active hostilities between Israel and Hamas.

Delivering France's national address later that day, President Macron rebuked that characterization, insisting that proclamations of diplomatic success were contradicted by the humanitarian reality on the ground. Macron stated that despite declarations of peace, not a single sustainable humanitarian corridor had been reopened to deliver essential food and medical relief supplies to civilians in the enclave. Addressing the assembly, Macron characterized the ongoing humanitarian deprivation as a "spectacle of shame," asking delegates: "Are we simply to stand by and watch this spectacle that should shame us all?"

In the same address, Macron warned that expanding settlement construction and escalating settler violence in the West Bank are actively eroding the territorial viability of a future Palestinian state, which he argued remains indispensable to long-term Israeli security. Macron spoke during the concluding phase of his second presidential term, which ends in May 2027, reiterating France's call for immediate, unhindered humanitarian access across Gaza.

Responding to Macron's remarks, White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly defended the administration's Middle East policies, stating that President Trump had established momentum toward ending active hostilities and was actively leading international efforts to execute the 20-Point Plan for Peace in Gaza. The exchange highlighted contrasting diplomatic approaches between Washington and Paris over international aid access and the political framework required to achieve regional stability.
