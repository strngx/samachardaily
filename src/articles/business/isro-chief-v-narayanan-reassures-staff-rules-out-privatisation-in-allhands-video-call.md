ISRO chairman V Narayanan convened a video conference with agency employees on Thursday after rumours of structural changes began circulating within the organisation. In the address, Narayanan categorically dismissed any move toward privatisation, assuring staff that the space agency will remain a fully government‑run entity.

The chairman then turned to the agency’s strategic roadmap, highlighting several forthcoming initiatives, including the next phase of the Gaganyaan crewed‑flight programme, expanded satellite launch services for commercial customers, and a new research partnership with the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology. Narayanan framed these steps as part of ISRO’s long‑term vision to deepen indigenous capability while maintaining public ownership.

Employees, however, used the forum to demand greater transparency on policy matters that affect day‑to‑day operations. Staff representatives pressed for explicit guidelines on industry collaboration, especially regarding joint ventures with private aerospace firms and the sharing of proprietary technologies. They also sought clarification on the criteria for accessing ISRO’s high‑end testing facilities and laboratories.

While the chairman answered several questions about the upcoming projects, many queries related to employee welfare—such as housing allowances, health benefits, and the use of on‑site amenities—were left without concrete responses. Observers noted that the lack of detail could fuel further uncertainty among a workforce that has recently expressed concerns over career progression and resource allocation.

Analysts view the meeting as a tactical move by ISRO’s leadership to quell speculation ahead of the agency’s upcoming budget review. By publicly rejecting privatisation and outlining a forward‑looking agenda, Narayanan aims to reinforce confidence among scientists, engineers, and support staff, while also signalling to the government that the agency remains aligned with national priorities.
