David Booth, the billionaire who co‑founded Dimensional Fund Advisors (DFA) in the early 1980s, is widely credited with bringing academic research on index investing to the mainstream. Drawing on the work of Nobel‑prize economists such as Eugene Fama and Kenneth French, Booth built a firm that offers diversified portfolios that track broad market factors while keeping costs low. Today, DFA manages several hundred billion dollars for institutional and retail clients worldwide, and Booth’s approach has helped shape the way millions of investors think about building wealth over decades.

In a recent interview, Booth distilled his decades of market experience into a single piece of advice: stay calm in the face of uncertainty. He warned that short‑term market swings often trigger emotional reactions—buying high out of optimism or selling low out of fear—that can erode long‑term returns. Instead, Booth recommends adhering to a disciplined, research‑backed plan, maintaining a diversified allocation, and resisting the urge to chase headlines or time the market. By focusing on the underlying drivers of returns rather than daily price movements, investors can stay aligned with their financial goals even when markets are turbulent.

Beyond investing, Booth applies the same principle to life. He describes his own routine—regular exercise, reading, and time with family—as a way to cultivate steadiness and perspective. His philanthropic work, including support for education and scientific research, reflects a belief that sustained, measured effort yields the most meaningful impact. Booth’s calm demeanor, both in business and personal pursuits, underscores his message that composure, not reaction, is the key to navigating uncertainty.
