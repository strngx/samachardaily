Edmunds, the automotive research firm, outlined four critical actions consumers should take before committing to a certified pre‑owned (CPO) car. While CPO vehicles typically command a 4%‑5% price premium over standard used cars, the added cost is meant to cover thorough factory‑trained technician inspections, manufacturer‑backed warranties and ancillary benefits such as roadside assistance.

First, buyers must obtain and scrutinize the detailed inspection report provided by the dealer. These reports list the specific components examined, any repairs performed, and the condition ratings for major systems, ensuring the vehicle meets the automaker’s stringent CPO standards.

Second, shoppers should verify the scope and duration of the warranty attached to the CPO vehicle. Edmunds stresses confirming whether the warranty mirrors the new‑car coverage or is a limited extension, and checking for any exclusions that could affect future repair costs.

Third, consumers are urged to compare the CPO price against comparable non‑certified used models. By calculating the 4%‑5% premium, buyers can determine if the added peace of mind justifies the extra expense, especially when factoring in potential resale value and depreciation trends.

Finally, Edmunds recommends confirming the inclusion and terms of roadside assistance. This service, often bundled with CPO purchases, can provide towing, lock‑out help and emergency fuel delivery, adding tangible value beyond the warranty.
