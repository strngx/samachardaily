U.S. equity benchmarks wrapped up August near record highs, and as trading desks turn the page to September, technicians are combing through charts for clues on whether the rally that has lifted the S&P 500, the Nasdaq 100, and the Toronto Stock Exchange Composite through the year can sustain its momentum. The starting point matters: the S&P 500 closed last month with a year-to-date gain of roughly 10%, while the Nasdaq 100 has stretched its leadership position, leaving portfolio managers increasingly attentive to overbought conditions and seasonal headwinds that historically weigh on U.S. stocks in September.

The Investing.com market analysis focuses on three pillars of the current setup — the Nasdaq 100, the S&P 500, and the West Texas Intermediate crude oil futures contract — because the same risk-on appetite driving tech megacaps has also lifted cyclical commodities. WTI crude has climbed back above key moving averages in recent sessions, reinforcing a reflationary tilt that has helped energy names contribute to the broader index advance. For chart watchers, the relationship between the equity benchmarks and crude is a useful barometer of whether the move is being driven by genuine growth optimism or simply by passive flows chasing momentum into the back half of the year.

Behind the price action sits a familiar late-cycle backdrop: the Federal Reserve has held its policy rate steady while signaling that the first cut of the cycle could arrive as soon as the September meeting, even as inflation gauges remain sticky in certain services categories. Corporate earnings for the second quarter came in better than feared, with technology and communication services again leading aggregate growth, while guidance from several large-cap firms has been cautious enough to keep valuation multiples from expanding aggressively. That tension — resilient earnings against a still-tight policy stance — is what analysts say is being priced into the tight trading ranges visible on weekly charts across major U.S. and Canadian indices.

For global allocators, the September setup is also shaped by parallel forces in commodities and currencies. A weaker U.S. dollar index has historically been a tailwind for both emerging-market equities and dollar-denominated commodities, and the recent correlation breakdown between bonds and stocks has forced pension funds and multi-asset funds to revisit hedging ratios. The Investing.com note highlights the technical signals embedded in the Nasdaq 100's relative strength against the S&P 500, the position of the S&P 500 relative to its 50- and 200-day moving averages, and whether crude oil's breakout can hold above prior resistance — three data points that, taken together, may determine whether September becomes another month of melt-up or the long-anticipated seasonal correction finally arrives.
