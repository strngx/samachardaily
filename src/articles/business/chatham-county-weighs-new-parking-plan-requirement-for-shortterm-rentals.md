The Chatham County Commission in Georgia is evaluating proposed regulatory amendments that would require short-term vacation rental (STVR) operators to submit dedicated off-street parking plans prior to receiving or renewing operating licenses.

The policy initiative, reported by regional news outlet WSAV-TV, represents the local municipal government's latest effort to balance neighborhood preservation with the county's thriving tourism economy, particularly across Tybee Island and unincorporated coastal communities near Savannah. Under the proposed ordinance, property owners seeking STVR registration would be required to document verified off-street parking spaces corresponding directly to the maximum guest occupancy of the residence.

Residents in high-density coastal neighborhoods have repeatedly voiced concerns regarding narrow residential streets being obstructed by visitors' vehicles, creating safety hazards for emergency fire and medical response vehicles. County planning officials noted that existing code enforcement mechanisms have struggled to address overcrowding complaints during peak holiday weekends without explicit, pre-certified parking layout documentation.

The county commission is currently conducting public hearings with neighborhood associations, local hospitality representatives, and property managers to refine the parking formula before holding a binding vote. If enacted, Chatham County would join a growing roster of coastal municipalities utilizing parking and zoning mandates to regulate short-term rental density.
