Nike has confirmed a partnership with Japanese‑inspired streetwear label Wagyu, headquartered in New York City, to release a new collaborative sneaker line. The collection will revolve around the Air Max 90, a model originally designed by Nike legend Tinker Hatfield, and is being positioned as a fresh take on the classic silhouette.

Wagyu, known for its bold graphics and culinary‑themed branding, has cultivated a niche following in the city’s streetwear circuit. The label’s Instagram handle @wagyu.jp signals a digital‑first approach, and its New York base places it at the crossroads of fashion, art, and sneaker culture, making the partnership a natural fit for both parties.

The Air Max 90 debuted in 1990 and quickly became one of Nike’s most enduring models, celebrated for its visible Air cushioning and versatile design. Tinker Hatfield, the shoe’s original architect, is credited with infusing the model with a blend of performance technology and street‑ready aesthetics, a legacy that continues to attract collaborators seeking to reinterpret the shoe for new audiences.

According to the announcement, the Wagyu‑Nike collection will be “centered” on the Air Max 90, suggesting that the label will apply its signature visual language—often referencing Japanese cuisine and high‑end dining—to the shoe’s upper, midsole and branding elements. While specific colorways and material details remain under wraps, the collaboration promises a blend of Wagyu’s culinary motif with Nike’s performance heritage.

Industry observers note that the partnership arrives at a time when sneaker collaborations are increasingly used to generate hype and drive limited‑edition sales. By aligning with a niche yet influential label like Wagyu, Nike aims to tap into a younger, fashion‑forward demographic while reinforcing the Air Max 90’s status as a timeless canvas for creative reinterpretation.
