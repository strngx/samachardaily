Shares in major European exchanges posted declines on Thursday, mirroring a broader pullback that began on Wall Street earlier in the day.

Asian equity indices also opened lower, with investors reacting to the same set of pressures that weighed on U.S. markets.

The common thread linking the downturn across continents was the resurgence of crude oil prices, which again traded above the $100 a barrel threshold.

Higher oil prices have historically added cost pressures for corporations and consumers, prompting market participants to reassess earnings outlooks and inflation expectations.

Analysts note that the simultaneous dip in U.S., European and Asian markets underscores the interconnected nature of global finance, where shifts in commodity prices can quickly ripple through equity valuations.

While the exact drivers of the Wall Street retreat were not detailed, the combination of a strong oil market and broader risk aversion appears to have set the tone for the day's trading worldwide.
