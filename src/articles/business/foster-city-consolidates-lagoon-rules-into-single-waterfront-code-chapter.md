Foster City announced that it is merging its long‑standing lagoon regulations into a single, comprehensive code chapter. The move bundles rules that have evolved over several decades into a unified framework that will govern the construction and alteration of docks, seawalls, property setbacks and view corridors for homes along the city’s waterfront.

The new chapter explicitly addresses four key elements of waterfront development. Docks and private piers will be subject to standardized design and safety criteria, while seawalls must meet updated structural and environmental standards. Setback requirements will define the minimum distance between new structures and the water’s edge, and view protections will preserve sightlines for existing and future waterfront residences.

Historically, Foster City’s lagoon policies have been scattered across multiple sections of the municipal code, creating a patchwork of requirements that developers and homeowners often found difficult to navigate. By consolidating these provisions, the city aims to eliminate inconsistencies and streamline the permitting process, offering clearer guidance to property owners and contractors alike.

City officials say the unified code reflects a balance between encouraging responsible waterfront development and safeguarding the community’s aesthetic and environmental interests. The inclusion of view protections, in particular, responds to long‑standing concerns from residents about preserving the visual character of the lagoon shoreline amid increasing construction activity.

Implementation of the new chapter will be accompanied by an outreach program to inform stakeholders of the revised requirements. The city plans to release detailed guidance documents and hold informational workshops, ensuring that developers, architects and homeowners understand the updated standards before submitting future project applications.
