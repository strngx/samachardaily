A widespread selloff in U.S. government bonds has driven benchmark Treasury yields sharply higher, lifting borrowing costs across international credit markets and tightening financial conditions for consumers and corporations, according to analysis reported by The Economic Times.

As bond prices fall and yields climb, commercial lending rates tied to benchmark sovereign paper are adjusting upward. The yield increase has pushed 30-year fixed mortgage rates, consumer auto loans, and corporate credit spreads higher, increasing debt servicing burdens for households and dampening corporate capital expenditure plans.

The escalation in bond yields stems from persistent inflationary pressures, resilient economic growth data, and expanded sovereign debt issuance to fund fiscal deficits. When yields on safe-haven U.S. paper rise, international institutional capital often shifts out of equities and emerging-market debt toward dollar assets, strengthening the U.S. Dollar index.

Financial analysts note that higher long-term yields complicate central bank policy globally. Developing market monetary authorities face added pressure to maintain higher domestic policy rates to prevent currency depreciation and counter imported inflation, constraining liquidity across private credit channels.
