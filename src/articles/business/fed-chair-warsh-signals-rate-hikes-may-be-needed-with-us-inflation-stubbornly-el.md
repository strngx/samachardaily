Jerome Warsh, who took over the Federal Reserve’s chairmanship earlier this month, addressed reporters on the sidelines of a banking conference, emphasizing that the fight against inflation continues to dominate the Fed’s agenda. While the headline inflation rate has shown modest moderation in recent months, it remains well above the central bank’s 2% target, prompting Warsh to signal that the policy‑making body is prepared to raise the federal funds rate further if price pressures do not ease more decisively. He noted that the current target range of 5.25%‑5.50% was set to bring inflation down, but the data still reflect “stubbornly elevated” price growth in key sectors such as housing, energy, and services.

Warsh’s remarks came after the latest Consumer Price Index report showed year‑over‑year inflation at 3.6%, a figure that, while lower than the peak seen in 2022, still exceeds the Fed’s comfort zone. He cautioned that the central bank would not hesitate to act pre‑emptively, adding that “the credibility of our mandate depends on delivering price stability.” Wall Street reacted positively to the clear communication, with the S&P 500 edging higher and Treasury yields stabilising, as investors interpreted the statement as a reassurance that the Fed is not abandoning its inflation‑focused stance.

The chair also highlighted the importance of monitoring wage growth and core services inflation, which have shown resilience despite broader price moderation. He underscored that any future rate adjustments would be data‑dependent, with the Fed’s policy committee reviewing economic indicators on a quarterly basis to determine the appropriate path forward.
