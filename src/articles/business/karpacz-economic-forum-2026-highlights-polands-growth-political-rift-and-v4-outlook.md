The 2026 Karpacz Economic Forum convened government leaders, central bankers, economists, and corporate executives in southwestern Poland to assess the nation’s macroeconomic trajectory, fiscal challenges, and the evolving geopolitical role of the Visegrád Group (V4).

Conference deliberations centered on Poland’s resilient economic performance within the European Union, with projected annual gross domestic product (GDP) growth hovering around 3.2%. Discussions highlighted substantial capital inflows into domestic manufacturing and digital infrastructure, alongside Poland’s national defense spending, which currently exceeds 4.2% of GDP to support comprehensive military modernization and regional border security.

The forum also addressed emerging policy friction and strategic alignment across the Visegrád Group, which comprises Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, and Hungary. Delegates debated divergent national timelines regarding European Union decarbonization mandates, the development of cross-border liquid natural gas (LNG) interconnector pipelines, and the modernization of vital North-South rail and highway transit corridors across Central and Eastern Europe.

In a featured panel interview at the forum, former Prime Minister Mateusz Morawiecki offered his perspective on maintaining regional economic competitiveness amid shifting European industrial policies. Morawiecki stressed that sustained capital investment in energy security and high-tech supply chains remains essential for preserving Central Europe’s economic momentum and fostering pragmatic cooperation among V4 member states.
