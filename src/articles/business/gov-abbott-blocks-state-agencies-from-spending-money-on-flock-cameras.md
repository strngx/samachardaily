Governor Greg Abbott on Monday issued an executive order prohibiting all Texas state agencies from further spending on the Flock camera system, a move that comes as the Texas Tribune prepares to publish an investigative report on the program’s financing.

The Tribune’s investigation, authored by reporter Ayden Runnels, indicates that a single state agency has already devoted a minimum of $30 million to constructing a statewide Flock surveillance network that records drivers on public roadways. The report, scheduled for release later this week, details contracts, procurement records and internal memos that trace the flow of funds into the project.

Flock cameras are AI‑enabled devices that capture images of moving vehicles and transmit data to a central monitoring hub. Proponents argue the technology enhances traffic safety and law‑enforcement efficiency, while critics warn it expands state surveillance and raises privacy concerns. The $30 million outlay represents one of the largest single‑program investments in automated traffic monitoring in Texas history.

Abbott’s order reflects a broader political pushback against what he described as “uncontrolled spending” and “potential overreach” by state officials. By freezing additional expenditures, the governor signals that any further expansion of the network will require explicit legislative approval and a transparent cost‑benefit analysis.

The Texas Tribune has said the forthcoming story will name the agency involved and outline how the funds were allocated, but it has not yet disclosed the agency’s response. Advocacy groups and civil‑liberties organizations have welcomed Abbott’s intervention, calling for a full audit of the program, while some law‑enforcement officials caution that halting funding could disrupt ongoing traffic‑safety initiatives.
