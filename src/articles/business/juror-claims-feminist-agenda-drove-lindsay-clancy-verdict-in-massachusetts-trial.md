During a post‑trial media circuit, juror Paula Devlin publicly stated that she and other members of the predominantly female jury approached the Lindsay Clancy murder case with a feminist perspective, describing the verdict as a potential turning point for women’s rights.

The case centered on Clancy’s conviction for the pre‑planned killings of her three children, with prosecutors presenting evidence that she coordinated her husband’s errands, used a navigation app to time his travel, and carried out the murders in the basement while later phoning him to feign normalcy; the defense argued a temporary postpartum psychosis.

Devlin also noted that the jury was not sequestered, allowing jurors to return home each night and interact with family, friends and online content, a factor critics say may have exposed them to external political pressure; the trial’s aftermath saw social‑media hashtags such as “Repeal the 19th” trend for several days.
