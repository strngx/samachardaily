Food Bank for NYC, the city’s largest hunger‑relief organization serving all five boroughs, issued a formal appeal to Congress on Sept. 9, urging lawmakers to postpone a proposed federal policy that would transfer a portion of the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) funding burden to state governments.

The organization warned that the cost‑shift, slated to take effect later this year, would force New York State and its municipalities to absorb billions of dollars in additional expenditures, straining already tight fiscal resources and potentially prompting cuts to other critical services.

According to the Food Bank’s statement, the delayed funding would also intensify pressure on local food banks, which have seen a 15 % rise in client visits since the start of 2024. The group estimates that without full federal emergency food assistance, an additional 200,000 New Yorkers could face food insecurity within the next six months.

The appeal cites recent data from the U.S. Department of Agriculture showing that SNAP participation in New York has climbed to 4.2 million households, a 9 % increase from the previous year. The Food Bank argues that a sudden state‑level cost assumption could disrupt the continuity of benefits, leading to gaps in nutrition support for vulnerable families.

Congressional sponsors of the cost‑shift have framed the move as a fiscal responsibility measure, but Food Bank for NYC contends that the timing is ill‑suited given the lingering economic fallout from the pandemic and rising inflation. The organization called for an immediate, fully funded emergency food assistance package to bridge any shortfall and protect food access across the city.
