The National Petroleum Council (NPC) released a report urging Congress to authorize a coordinated national program that would map the United States’ subsurface resources in detail. The recommendation is framed as a strategic move to secure domestic supply chains for critical energy and mineral commodities, reducing reliance on foreign sources.

The NPC, an industry‑backed advisory body that provides long‑term strategic guidance to the U.S. government on petroleum and related sectors, argues that a comprehensive geological survey would identify untapped oil, natural gas, and critical mineral deposits. By creating a unified data set, the council says policymakers could make more informed decisions about resource development, infrastructure investment, and environmental stewardship.

Currently, subsurface data in the United States is fragmented across federal, state, and private entities, often collected for specific projects rather than as part of a national inventory. The NPC’s report highlights that this patchwork approach hampers the ability to assess the full scope of domestic resources, especially as global supply chains face increasing volatility. A coordinated mapping effort, the council suggests, would fill those gaps and provide a baseline for future extraction and processing activities.

The council’s recommendation comes amid broader governmental initiatives aimed at bolstering energy independence and critical mineral security. While the report does not specify funding levels, it calls for a clear legislative mandate that would enable the Department of the Interior, the U.S. Geological Survey, and other relevant agencies to work together under a unified framework. The NPC stresses that timely action is essential to keep pace with international competitors that are already expanding their own subsurface exploration programs.

Stakeholders, including industry groups, environmental organizations, and state governments, are expected to weigh in as the proposal moves through the legislative process. The NPC emphasizes that transparent data sharing and rigorous scientific standards will be key to balancing economic development with environmental protection.
