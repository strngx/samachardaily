On Thursday, August 28, the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) threatened to pull its players from the field after the lunch interval of the second Test against England at Lord’s. The trigger was a live interview aired by the host broadcaster Sky Sports that featured Imran Khan’s two sons, who discussed political matters unrelated to cricket. The PCB argued that the segment breached the International Cricket Council’s (ICC) rule prohibiting political commentary during matches and that it undermined the integrity of the series. Despite repeated requests from the PCB to edit or remove the interview, Sky Sports continued to broadcast the full segment, prompting the board to warn that the team would not take the field after lunch if the broadcast was not curtailed.

The controversy unfolded as the Pakistani side had already endured a challenging first Test, losing by an innings. Team management, led by head coach Grant Bradburn, was caught between respecting the board’s directive and maintaining the players’ focus on the match. After a brief deliberation, PCB officials decided to allow the team to resume play, citing the need to avoid further disruption to the series and the potential sanctions that a walk‑out could attract from the ICC. The match proceeded, but the incident sparked a heated debate among fans, commentators and cricket administrators about the boundaries of media freedom, political content and the responsibilities of broadcasters during international fixtures.
