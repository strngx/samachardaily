Manchester United Women began their new season on a sour note as they fell 2-1 to London City Lionesses, prompting a sharp rebuke from newly appointed manager Eva Olid. The Spanish coach, who took charge ahead of the new campaign, made clear after the match that the performance was not reflective of the identity she is trying to instill at the club. Olid told reporters that her players simply were not themselves for the duration of the contest, a stinging assessment delivered on the back of a result that will heap early pressure on her tenure.

The defeat will come as a jolt to a Manchester United Women squad that has grown accustomed to competing at the sharp end of the Women's Super League. London City Lionesses, long established as one of the division's more stubborn opponents, took full advantage of a flat visiting side to claim all three points at home. United did manage to find the net through a consolation effort, but the wider display left Olid searching for answers, particularly given the expectations surrounding a fresh managerial era and the optimism generated around the club's preseason preparations.

Olid's appointment had been viewed internally as a signal of intent, with the club seeking a sharper, more tactically defined identity after a campaign that fell short of the standards set earlier in the decade. Her post-match comments suggest she will demand immediate improvement rather than allow the opening-day setback to metastasize into a longer trend. For United, the fixture list offers little respite, and how the squad responds in the next outing will be the first serious test of Olid's influence in the dressing room.

The Lionesses, meanwhile, will take significant encouragement from a win that underscores their capacity to trouble the league's marquee sides on their own patch. For Olid and Manchester United Women, the focus now shifts rapidly to recovery, regrouping, and ensuring that the frustrations of this opening fixture are not repeated when the schedule returns. The manager's pointed public critique suggests she is unwilling to tolerate anything less than a full response from her players the next time they take the field.
