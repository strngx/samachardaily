Los Angeles Rams head coach Sean McVay expects both star wide receiver Puka Nacua and left tackle Alaric Jackson to be available when his team opens the NFL season against the San Francisco 49ers on Thursday in Melbourne, Australia. Speaking to reporters Sunday, McVay said he does not believe either player will be suspended for the Week 1 matchup, despite ongoing off-field issues involving both players. The Rams and 49ers will meet at a neutral venue in Melbourne to kick off the new season, marking one of the league's marquee international games.

Nacua, one of the league's premier young wide receivers, and Jackson, a key piece of Los Angeles' offensive line, had both been connected to separate off-field matters in recent weeks. The nature of those incidents has drawn attention around the franchise as it prepared to travel abroad for the high-profile opener. McVay's public comments signal that the Rams are operating under the assumption that any league discipline, if it comes, will arrive after the team has already played its first game.

Melbourne's hosting of a regular-season NFL game reflects the league's continued international expansion, with Australia now part of a slate that includes fixtures in London, Munich, and other cities. For the Rams, the trip represents both a competitive challenge and a logistical one, requiring the organization to manage player availability, travel, and preparation across a significant time difference. McVay's update on Sunday addressed one of the most immediate concerns facing the team as it works toward Thursday's kickoff.

The Rams and 49ers, long-standing NFC West rivals, will square off in a game that carries early playoff-implication weight within the division. San Francisco is coming off a stretch of deep postseason runs, while Los Angeles has reshaped parts of its roster during the offseason. With Nacua and Jackson now expected to play, the Rams will deploy their top pass-catching option and starting left tackle against a 49ers defense that ranked among the league's most disruptive units last season.

McVay's remarks leave open the possibility that the league could still announce discipline later in the season if either player's off-field situation warrants further review. For now, though, Los Angeles is planning around a full complement of starters on both sides of the ball as it prepares to face one of its fiercest rivals more than 15,000 kilometres from SoFi Stadium.
