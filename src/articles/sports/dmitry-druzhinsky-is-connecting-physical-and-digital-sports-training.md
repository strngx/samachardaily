New York‑based sports tech entrepreneur Dmitry Druzhinsky, co‑founder of MatchPoint NYC, announced the rollout of an online training initiative designed to integrate physical sports environments with digital technology. The platform, built on MatchPoint’s existing network of courts and coaching staff, offers athletes a suite of video‑based drills, performance‑tracking dashboards and remote coaching sessions that can be accessed from any device. By digitising the pre‑ and post‑session analysis, the service allows users to review technique, receive data‑driven feedback, and customise workout plans without the need to be on a physical court for every step of their development.

The launch comes as the sports industry continues to adapt lessons learned from recent disruptions that forced many training programs online. Druzhinsky highlighted that the initiative does not aim to replace traditional facilities but to complement them, creating a hybrid model where athletes can maximise time on the field while leveraging technology for continuous improvement. Early adopters, ranging from youth basketball teams to adult fitness enthusiasts, will pilot the platform’s interactive modules, which include AI‑assisted movement analysis and virtual reality simulations of game scenarios. MatchPoint NYC plans to expand the offering to additional sports and partner with schools and community centres to broaden its reach.

While the service is currently centred on the U.S. market, Druzhinsky indicated intentions to scale internationally, noting the universal demand for flexible, data‑rich training solutions. The initiative also aligns with broader trends in sports tech, where wearable sensors, cloud analytics and streaming content are reshaping how athletes train, recover and compete.
