Newcastle United confirmed on transfer deadline day that they have set a concrete asking price for teenage striker Lewis Hall, signalling that the club is prepared to negotiate a sale if the right offer arrives. The Magpies’ statement indicated that Manchester United are one of three Premier League sides monitoring the situation, each hoping to secure the forward before the window shuts.

Hall, 19, has emerged as one of Newcastle’s most promising attacking talents this season, contributing eight goals and several assists across all competitions. His performances have attracted interest from several top clubs, but the Magpies have consistently emphasized his long‑term value to the squad. The disclosed price range, reported to be between £60 million and £75 million, reflects both his potential and the market premium placed on young English forwards.

Manchester United, fresh from the acquisition of Carlos Baleba, are reportedly keen to add Hall to bolster their attacking options. Former United captain Bryan Robson publicly urged the club’s hierarchy to act quickly, describing Hall as a "£60 million star" who could complement United’s evolving front line. United’s scouting department is said to have three shortlist options for a last‑minute signing, with Hall topping the list due to his versatility and Premier League experience.

Newcastle’s management, led by manager Eddie Howe, has made it clear that any deal must meet the club’s valuation and include clauses that protect their future interests. The price tag is intended to reflect Hall’s projected market value and to fund reinvestment in the squad, particularly in areas weakened by recent departures. Howe has also hinted that a substantial bid could trigger a release clause, though the exact terms remain confidential.

The broader transfer market has been marked by frantic activity as clubs scramble to finalize deals before the deadline. Hall’s potential move underscores the premium placed on home‑grown talent and the willingness of elite clubs to spend heavily on promising prospects. If United succeed, it would be one of the last high‑profile signings of the window, while Newcastle would retain a valuable asset or secure a sizable fee to reinvest.
