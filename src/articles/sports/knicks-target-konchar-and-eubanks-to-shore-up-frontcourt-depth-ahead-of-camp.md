The New York Knicks are actively exploring options to fortify their frontcourt roster ahead of the upcoming training camp, focusing interest on free agent forwards John Konchar and Drew Eubanks. Both players present experienced rotational insurance as the front office evaluates potential late-offseason additions to solidify its depth chart.

Konchar enters the open market following a brief stint with the Minnesota Timberwolves, who recently waived and stretched his remaining salary after acquiring him via trade. Known for his tactical flexibility and rebounding from the perimeter, Konchar provides an intriguing wing-forward option for a New York squad seeking reliable bench support capable of slotting into multiple defensive schemes.

Eubanks, meanwhile, is seeking to resume his career after his 2025-26 campaign with the Sacramento Kings cut short prematurely. The veteran big man was forced to the sidelines in March after undergoing surgical repair on a torn ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) in his left thumb. If finalized, a potential deal with New York would place Eubanks directly in competition with the team's existing reserve frontcourt for the third center position.

However, financial restrictions dictated by the league's collective bargaining framework strictly cap New York's operational flexibility. The Knicks currently possess enough room beneath the second tax apron to add only a single player on a veteran minimum contract. The organization lacks the mathematical threshold required to sign both free agents without crossing critical hard-cap boundaries.

Knicks owner James Dolan has publicly declared that the franchise will under no circumstances exceed the second tax apron, which is capped at $221 million in total team salary for the current season. As a result, executive decision-makers must carefully determine whether Konchar's positional versatility or Eubanks' interior rim protection best fits the club's remaining operational needs.
