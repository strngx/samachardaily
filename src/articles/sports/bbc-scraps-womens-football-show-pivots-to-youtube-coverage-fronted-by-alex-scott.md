The British Broadcasting Corporation has quietly axed the Women's Football Show ahead of the new season, bringing an end to the long-standing television program. However, far from stepping back from the sport, the public broadcaster is actually scaling up its total coverage of women's football. As part of this comprehensive broadcast shift, the network is launching a brand-new YouTube channel, placing prominent presenter and former player Alex Scott at the forefront of its online video programming.

This major realignment in broadcasting strategy arrives just as the domestic campaign gets underway this weekend with the return of the Women's Super League. Top-flight domestic action kicks off on Friday night when London City Lionesses take on Manchester United, launching a competitive slate of weekend fixtures that will set the tone for English women's football over the coming months.

While the removal of the dedicated Women's Football Show from linear television schedules marks a significant shift for traditional viewers, the BBC's strategy is explicitly designed to build momentum ahead of next year's FIFA Women's World Cup in Brazil. By reallocating broadcast resources into digital-first channels, the network intends to deliver expanded content to a wider audience across online platforms in the lead-up to the global tournament.

By putting Alex Scott at the helm of the newly established YouTube channel, the BBC is leveraging one of its most recognizable sports presenters to drive digital growth. The transition highlights a clear industry pivot toward accessible, on-demand video formats designed to capture football fans who increasingly prefer streaming content over scheduled late-night television programming during the Women's Super League season.

As London City Lionesses and Manchester United prepare to open the domestic campaign on Friday night, the BBC's new digital direction represents a pivotal operational adjustment. The public broadcaster aims to ensure that fans follow all the action from the Women's Super League through its expanded online footprint as anticipation continues to build for next year's marquee World Cup in South America.
