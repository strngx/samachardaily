Angel Reese has spoken of her excitement at finally linking up with Breanna Stewart as the duo prepare to launch Team USA's World Cup campaign against China in the opening game of the tournament. Speaking ahead of the fixture, the Chicago Sky forward described Stewart as a player she has long admired and said the prospect of suiting up with her on the senior national team stage was a career milestone she had been looking forward to. The pairing brings together two of the most recognizable faces in the WNBA, with Reese a marquee attraction of the league's newest generation and Stewart already established as one of the most decorated players in the sport.

Stewart's status as 'absolute WNBA royalty,' as described in coverage of the squad, underlines why her presence alongside Reese carries such weight for the United States. The Seattle Storm forward has won MVP honours, championships and Finals MVP awards, and her international résumé includes Olympic gold medals and prior World Cup experience. For Reese, who has rapidly emerged as a cultural phenomenon since bursting into the league, sharing a frontcourt rotation with Stewart represents a bridge from emerging star to fully-fledged national team contributor, with the world's No. 1 ranked side offering the highest possible platform to showcase that growth.

The opening assignment against China gives the Americans an early test of their medal credentials. China have historically been one of the few nations capable of pushing the United States in major competitions, and while the gap between the two programmes has narrowed and widened across different cycles, any opening-day fixture at a World Cup sets the tone for a tournament run. Team USA's coaching staff will be keen to assess combinations, rotations and on-court chemistry in that first contest, making the Reese-Stewart axis one of several that will be under the spotlight as the tournament tips off.

Beyond the tactical dimension, the storyline carries commercial and cultural significance for women's basketball. Reese's profile extends well beyond traditional sports audiences, while Stewart's résumé gives the team a veteran anchor whose competitiveness is rarely in question. Together, their union in the national team setup reflects how the WNBA's generational handoff is unfolding in real time on one of the biggest international stages the sport offers, with the wider women's game continuing to ride a surge in visibility heading into the tournament.
