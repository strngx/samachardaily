Mayo football is turning to senior Kellen Burger as its starting quarterback, a move that underscores both the program's youth and its willingness to deploy a two-way player in the most physical position on the field. Burger, described by Post Bulletin as the rare linebacker-quarterback combo, will lead a squad that is otherwise light on upperclassmen and experience.

The senior's profile is unconventional for the position. Most high school quarterbacks are evaluated first on footwork, release and decision-making, with contact seen as something to avoid. Burger inverts that standard, splitting time at linebacker and operating in the middle of the defense, where collisions are part of the job. The arrangement speaks to a player whose identity is built around physicality rather than around the traditional pocket-passer mold.

For Mayo, the decision reflects the realities of a young roster. When a program lacks depth at quarterback, coaches often look for an athlete whose skill set translates to multiple positions, and Burger fits that mold. His linebacker background gives him a defender's read on offensive tendencies, while his size and willingness to absorb contact could be an asset in short-yardage and red-zone situations.

The move also places a leadership burden on Burger, who as a senior becomes the most experienced voice in a developing locker room. Mayo will lean on his presence as much as his production, particularly early in the season when younger teammates are still adjusting to varsity speed. The team will be looking to its quarterback not only to manage drives but to set the physical tone that has defined his career on the other side of the ball.
