Oklahoma State University announced the selection of seven senior leaders to serve as team captains for the 2026 football season. The decision, made by the coaching staff and senior players, reflects a blend of on‑field performance, locker‑room influence and the program’s strategic recruiting focus.

Captains in college football are tasked with setting the tone during practices, representing the squad in media briefings and serving as the primary liaison between the coaching staff and the roster. At a program that consistently competes for conference honors, the role carries added weight, especially as the Cowboys prepare for a demanding schedule that includes the storied Bedlam rivalry against the University of Oklahoma.

A notable feature of this year’s leadership group is that the majority of the captains come from the North Texas region, underscoring the deepening pipeline between Oklahoma State and the talent‑rich markets of the Dallas‑Fort Worth corridor. Recruiters have long praised the area for producing versatile athletes, and the current cohort of captains exemplifies that trend, bringing a mix of size, speed and football IQ that the Cowboys hope will translate into on‑field success.

Adding an international dimension, one of the newly named captains is an Australian-born player who earned a scholarship after impressing at a junior showcase in Melbourne. His inclusion highlights the program’s expanding scouting reach and the growing presence of Australian athletes in American college football, particularly at the punter and kicker positions.

The leadership group will assume its duties immediately, guiding the team through spring practices, summer conditioning and the high‑stakes Bedlam matchup that traditionally caps the regular season. Coaches expect the captains to foster cohesion among a roster that blends home‑grown Texas talent with out‑of‑state and overseas recruits, aiming to sustain the Cowboys’ competitive edge in the conference.
