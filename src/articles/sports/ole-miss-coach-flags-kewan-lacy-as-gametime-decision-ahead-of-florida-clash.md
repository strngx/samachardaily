Ole Miss running back Kewan Lacy was initially considered a game-time decision for the Rebels’ SEC matchup against Florida after suffering a left shoulder injury in the team’s 32-24 victory over LSU.

Head coach Pete Golding provided an encouraging update on Lacy’s condition, saying the injury was not as serious as initially feared. Lacy underwent an MRI after leaving the LSU game, with the results reportedly showing that he would not require surgery.

Despite the positive medical update, Golding said Lacy would have to work through the week before a final decision was made on whether he could play against the Gators.

## Lacy Injured Against LSU

Lacy suffered the injury during the third quarter of Ole Miss’ win over LSU on September 19.

The junior running back had already made an impact before leaving the game, rushing for 43 yards and a touchdown on nine carries. He landed hard on his left shoulder during a tackle and did not return for the remainder of the contest.

Ole Miss initially feared the injury could be significant, but postgame X-rays were negative. Golding later said the MRI results were more positive than expected.

Lacy was going through a rehabilitation program during the week, with the coaching staff monitoring his practice workload before determining whether he could play.

## Same Shoulder Had Previous Injury

The situation was particularly notable because Lacy had undergone offseason surgery on the same shoulder following an injury suffered during Ole Miss' College Football Playoff run the previous season.

That made the Rebels cautious about rushing him back onto the field.

Lacy had also dealt with another injury earlier in the 2026 season. He suffered a thigh bruise during Ole Miss' season-opening win over Louisville and missed part of that game before returning to action the following week.

Despite those setbacks, Lacy remained one of the most important players in the Ole Miss offense.

## Lacy's Importance to Ole Miss

Lacy entered the 2026 season after one of the best rushing campaigns in Ole Miss history.

In 2025, he rushed for 1,567 yards and 24 touchdowns, setting a single-season school record for rushing yards and leading the Southeastern Conference in rushing touchdowns.

His combination with quarterback Trinidad Chambliss was a major part of Ole Miss' run to the College Football Playoff semifinals.

Through the first three games of the 2026 season, Lacy had recorded 191 rushing yards and four touchdowns on 39 carries.

His availability therefore represented a major question heading into the matchup with Florida.

## Ole Miss Has Running Back Depth

The Rebels did have alternatives if Lacy was unable to play.

Ole Miss added multiple transfer running backs during the offseason to strengthen its depth at the position. JT Lindsey, a former LSU transfer, received additional opportunities after Lacy left the LSU game.

Lindsey finished the game with four carries for 20 yards and also contributed on a successful two-point conversion.

Michigan State transfer Makhi Frazier was another option in the backfield. Frazier had missed the LSU game after suffering an injury during practice but was expected to be available against Florida.

Joshua Dye, another transfer running back, also provided additional depth for the Rebels.

## Florida Matchup Adds Pressure

The uncertainty surrounding Lacy came ahead of one of Ole Miss' biggest early-season tests.

Ole Miss entered the matchup ranked No. 4 nationally, while Florida was ranked No. 21. Both teams were unbeaten heading into the SEC contest.

The game was scheduled for Saturday afternoon in Gainesville, putting Ole Miss on the road against a ranked conference opponent.

Without Lacy, the Rebels would have been forced to rely more heavily on their other running backs while putting additional responsibility on quarterback Trinidad Chambliss and the passing game.

With Lacy available, Ole Miss would have had its leading returning rushing threat to complement Chambliss.

## SEC Availability Reports

The Southeastern Conference uses formal availability reports to provide information about player status before games.

Players can initially be listed under designations including available, probable, questionable, doubtful or out. On game day, the final categories include available, game-time decision and out.

Lacy's status was therefore expected to become clearer as the week progressed.

Golding's initial assessment gave the Rebels some optimism, but the coaching staff still needed to determine whether Lacy could handle contact and a meaningful workload without aggravating the shoulder.

## A Careful Approach From Ole Miss

The Rebels had little reason to rush Lacy back if he was not fully ready.

The injury occurred early enough in the season that Ole Miss had to consider the potential impact of aggravating the shoulder over the remainder of the schedule.

Lacy's previous importance to the offense also meant that the team needed to balance the value of having him available against the possibility of making the injury worse.

Golding's comments suggested that Ole Miss would monitor Lacy's progress throughout the week rather than make an immediate decision.

## What Was Known Before the Florida Game

At the time of Golding's initial update, Lacy's MRI had provided encouraging news and he was not expected to require surgery.

However, he remained a game-time decision for the Florida matchup.

The Rebels had several alternatives available in the backfield, but none had Lacy's recent production or established role in the offense.

His final availability was therefore one of the biggest pregame questions surrounding the Ole Miss-Florida matchup.

The decision was ultimately expected to come after the team evaluated his rehabilitation and practice work and determined whether he could safely take the field.
