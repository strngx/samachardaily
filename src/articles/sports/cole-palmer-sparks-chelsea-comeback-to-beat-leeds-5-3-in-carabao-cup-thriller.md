Chelsea rescued a precarious Carabao Cup third‑round tie against Leeds United on Tuesday, overturning a 2-0 first‑half deficit to win 5-3 after a dramatic second‑half surge. Cole Palmer opened the comeback with a goal in the 55th minute and added a second four minutes later, setting the tone for a rapid four‑goal flurry that left Leeds reeling.

Leeds had taken the initiative early, with Tarik Muharemovic heading in from a set piece in the 12th minute and Brenden Aaronson doubling the lead from open play in the 27th. The visitors appeared in control, and Chelsea’s manager Xabi Alonso faced criticism for his heavily rotated side, which many pundits feared would struggle against a disciplined Leeds defence.

Alonso’s gamble paid off when he introduced Morgan Rogers and Pedro Neto at halftime, followed by a fourth substitution that reshaped the midfield. The changes sparked a shift in tempo, and Chelsea pressed high, forcing errors that led to Palmer’s first strike. Within the next 12 minutes, Chelsea added three more goals – a brace from Palmer, a strike by Rogers and a late clincher from Neto – to seal the win.

The victory marks Chelsea’s first progression past the third round in the competition in 14 of the last 18 attempts, a statistic that underscores the club’s recent struggles in cup tournaments. With no European football this season, the Carabao Cup offers a rare opportunity for silverware, and the win restores some optimism among a squad that has been described as chaotic under Alonso’s early tenure.

Leeds, now eliminated, will reflect on missed chances to extend their cup run, while Chelsea will look to build momentum ahead of their upcoming Premier League fixtures. The match also highlighted the impact of in‑game tactical adjustments, reinforcing Alonso’s willingness to make bold substitutions when the game hangs in the balance.
