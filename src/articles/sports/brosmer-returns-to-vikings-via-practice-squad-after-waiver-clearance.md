The Minnesota Vikings have brought quarterback Brosmer back onto their practice squad, a move that comes just after the former Gophers signal‑caller cleared waivers. The team announced that Brosmer, who was released on Monday, successfully passed through the waiver period without being claimed by any other club, allowing him to return to the organization that originally signed him. His return provides the team with additional quarterback depth as they prepare for the remainder of the season.

Brosmer played his college football for the Minnesota Gophers, where he served as the starting quarterback for several seasons. After going undrafted, he signed with the Vikings as a free agent and spent time on their active roster before being moved to the practice squad earlier in the year. His release on Monday came as part of the team's roster adjustments. The decision to part ways with him was viewed as a move to create space for other players on the roster.

When a player is released, they enter the waiver system, which gives other teams a limited window to claim them. If no team files a claim, the player clears waivers and becomes eligible to sign with any team, including the one that released him. In Brosmer's case, the lack of a claim allowed the Vikings to re‑sign him to their practice squad without facing competition from other clubs. This process is a standard part of roster management in professional football.

The practice squad serves as a reserve list where players can develop their skills and stay ready for potential call‑ups to the active roster. It allows teams to retain talent that may not be ready for game action but still possesses value. By placing Brosmer on the practice squad, the Vikings keep a familiar quarterback in their system while maintaining flexibility to elevate him if needed. This arrangement also provides an opportunity for him to work closely with coaches and refine his preparation.

Rejoining the practice squad positions Brosmer for a potential return to the active roster should an injury create an opening. The move also signals that the organization still believes in his abilities and sees a future role for him within the team's quarterback depth. As the season progresses, his performance in practice could influence whether he earns a spot on game day. In the meantime, he will continue to contribute to the team's preparation and serve as a reliable backup option.
