The 2026 World Rowing Championships, staged on the calm waters of Račice in the Czech Republic, saw a dramatic showdown in the women's double sculls final. Ireland’s Zoe Hyde and Margaret Cremen powered their boat to a time of 6:42.48, edging out a field of seasoned competitors to claim the top podium spot. The pair displayed seamless coordination from the start, maintaining a high stroke rate through the middle 1,000 metres before accelerating in the final sprint to seal the victory.

Hyde, a veteran of the senior squad, and Cremen, a rising talent from the national development programme, had been paired earlier in the season as part of Rowing Ireland’s strategic push for world‑class performance. Their chemistry was evident throughout the regatta, with both athletes delivering consistent splits that kept them in contention from the first 500 metres. In the closing 500 metres, they surged ahead, crossing the finish line just ahead of the nearest rivals, confirming a well‑executed race plan.

The win marks a watershed moment for Irish rowing, as it is the first time an Irish crew has topped the podium in the women's double sculls at a world championship. The achievement follows a series of strong showings by Irish crews in recent years, underscoring the effectiveness of the federation’s investment in high‑performance training and talent identification. Hyde and Cremen’s gold adds to a growing medal tally for Ireland, which also includes successes in lightweight and sweep events earlier in the week.
