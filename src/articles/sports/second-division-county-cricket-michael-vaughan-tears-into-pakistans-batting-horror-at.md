Former England captain Michael Vaughan delivered a scathing assessment of Pakistan’s batting lineup following a dramatic top-order collapse during their match at Lord's. Vaughan drew a sharp comparison between the international side's performance and lower-tier domestic cricket, remarking that the display resembled 'second division county cricket.' His remarks came after several established senior batters failed to construct meaningful partnerships against the opposition bowling attack.

The foundation of Pakistan’s batting innings crumbled early as their core top-order collapsed in quick succession. Abdullah Shafique was dismissed after contributing just 5 runs, while captain Shan Masood managed only 1 run before returning to the pavilion. Star batsman Babar Azam was unable to stabilize the innings, departing after scoring 8 runs, and wicketkeeper-batter Mohammad Rizwan followed shortly after with just 7 runs to his name. The string of single-digit scores left Pakistan's middle and lower order severely exposed under heavy pressure at the historic venue.

Amid the widespread batting failure, opening batsman Azan Awais provided the sole notable resistance for Pakistan. Awais top-scored for the side with a disciplined knock of 46 runs, showing composure while senior international stars folded around him. However, his efforts were insufficient to rebuild the team's total, as the lack of support from the established top four halted any potential momentum or recovery.

Vaughan's public criticism underscores growing concerns surrounding Pakistan's top-order consistency when facing challenging conditions abroad. The failure of experienced campaigners like Babar, Masood, and Rizwan in the same innings highlighted persistent tactical and technical vulnerabilities. Pundits and former cricketers have increasingly questioned the team's ability to handle quality bowling spells in high-stakes fixtures, particularly at iconic grounds such as Lord's.

The batting collapse adds further scrutiny to Pakistan's ongoing international campaign, putting extra pressure on team management and coaching staff to address structural shortcomings in the top order. With crucial fixtures remaining, the side faces an urgent task to rectify its technical flaws, restore batting discipline, and ensure that key senior players find form ahead of upcoming matches.
