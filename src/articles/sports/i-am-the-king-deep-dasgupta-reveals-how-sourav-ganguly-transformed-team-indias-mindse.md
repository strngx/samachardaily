Dasgupta, speaking to Swapupdate, recalled the early 2000s when Ganguly took over a side that often bowed to verbal intimidation from opponents. The former captain introduced a no‑apology stance, urging players to meet sledging head‑on and use it as motivation rather than a distraction.

He highlighted specific instances—such as the 2002 NatWest Series final and the 2003 World Cup—where Ganguly’s insistence on standing tall helped younger players like Rahul Dravid and Virender Sehwag develop mental toughness. Dasgupta said the cultural shift was as much about body language and team meetings as it was about on‑field tactics.

Dasgupta also noted that the mindset change extended beyond sledging. Under Ganguly, the team embraced a more attacking style, backing themselves to chase big totals and play expansive cricket. This confidence, he argues, laid the groundwork for India’s later successes, including the 2011 World Cup triumph.
