Nebraska Athletics presented a formal proposal to shift the city’s convention center to a site directly adjacent to Memorial Stadium, the historic home of the Cornhuskers football program. The plan was delivered to Governor Jim Pillen in a briefing that highlighted the potential for a seamless integration of sports, tourism and business events on the university’s campus.

According to the department’s pitch, the new location would capitalize on the stadium’s existing infrastructure, parking capacity and high‑visibility profile. Proponents argue that event organizers could benefit from immediate access to the stadium’s amenities, while the university could generate additional revenue streams from conventions, trade shows and corporate gatherings held on campus.

Financing the relocation, the proposal suggests, could be achieved through a turnback tax – a fiscal tool that redirects a portion of state sales‑tax receipts back into a designated project. Officials indicated that the tax would be structured to avoid burdening taxpayers while providing a reliable revenue source for construction and long‑term maintenance of the facility.

The current convention center sits in downtown Lincoln, a location that has served the city for decades but faces constraints such as limited expansion space and aging utilities. Relocating the center to the stadium’s perimeter would free up valuable downtown real estate and potentially spur new development, according to the athletic department’s feasibility analysis.

Stakeholders across the political and business spectrum have begun weighing the proposal’s merits. City council members, local chambers of commerce and community groups are expected to review the economic impact studies, traffic projections and environmental assessments before any formal decision is made.
