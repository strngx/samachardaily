Theodora Petalas, who previously dated Greek tennis star Stefanos Tsitsipas, took to social media on Monday to denounce Naomi Osaka's outfit for the US Open. In a brief post, Petalas described the attire as "distracting" for Osaka's opponent, suggesting that the design could affect on‑court focus. Her comments arrived just hours after Osaka stepped onto Arthur Ashe Stadium in a high‑visibility ensemble that has already sparked conversation among fans and commentators.

Osaka, a four‑time Grand Slam champion, has previously faced scrutiny for her fashion choices on the court, most notably the controversial “Black Lives Matter” shirt she wore at the 2020 US Open and the bold neon tracksuit she debuted at the 2022 tournament. This latest outfit, featuring a bright, patterned top paired with a contrasting skirt, continues the trend of athletes using apparel to make personal statements, a practice that has drawn both praise and criticism within the tennis community.

Petalas' remarks echo a broader dialogue about the role of clothing in professional tennis. The sport’s governing bodies, including the United States Tennis Association (USTA), maintain a dress code that permits a range of styles but also emphasizes that attire should not interfere with play. While there is no formal rule prohibiting eye‑catching designs, players and officials have occasionally raised concerns when outfits are deemed overly flamboyant or potentially disruptive.

The former girlfriend of Tsitsipas, who himself is a top‑10 player and a regular fixture at the US Open, has not previously been a vocal commentator on tennis fashion. Her sudden entry into the debate appears motivated by the high‑profile nature of Osaka’s match against a seeded opponent, though she offered no further elaboration beyond the initial tweet. The post quickly garnered attention, with several tennis analysts noting that personal relationships often color public commentary in the sport.

As the tournament progresses, Osaka remains focused on her performance, having advanced to the third round after a straight‑sets victory. Whether her outfit will become a talking point beyond Petalas' critique remains uncertain, but the incident underscores the persistent tension between player self‑expression and the traditional expectations of tennis decorum.
