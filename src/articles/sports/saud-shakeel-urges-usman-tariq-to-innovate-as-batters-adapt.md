Pakistan middle-order batter Saud Shakeel has said that opposition batters have worked out how to prepare for spinner Usman Tariq, and that the off-spinner now needs to bring something new to his bowling if he wants to stay effective in international cricket. The comments were reported on 29 September 2026, with Shakeel quoted through a post on X by journalist Arfa Feroz Zake. They come less than a year after Tariq's international debut, a period in which he has become one of the most talked-about bowlers in Pakistan's white-ball set-up.

## What Saud Shakeel Said About Usman Tariq

Saud Shakeel is a left-handed batter who has played Test cricket for Pakistan and bats in the middle order. He gave a candid view of Tariq's standing in the game. According to the report, Shakeel believes the element of surprise that helped Tariq make an immediate impact has begun to fade, because batters now do their homework before they face him.

In the quote attributed to him, Shakeel said batters now "come prepared" against the bowler, and that Tariq will have to do something different to prove himself at the highest level. The remarks were relayed through a social media post rather than a full interview, so the wider conversation around them has not been published in full.

## How Usman Tariq Made His Mark in International Cricket

Tariq made his T20 International debut against South Africa in Lahore in November 2025 [VERIFY: exact date of debut match]. Squad announcements at the time listed him as the only uncapped player in Pakistan's T20I group for that home series. He kept his place for the T20I tour of Sri Lanka in January 2026, a trip that was part of Pakistan's preparation for the ICC Men's T20 World Cup 2026.

The World Cup was played across India and Sri Lanka, and Pakistan played all of their matches in Colombo. Tariq was part of that campaign. He took 3/27 from four overs in Pakistan's 32-run win over the United States, and his best T20I figures, 4/16, came against Namibia in the same tournament.

Through 2026 he has also kept busy in franchise cricket. According to the report on Shakeel's comments, he played in the Pakistan Super League, The Hundred and the Caribbean Premier League (CPL) this year, which has kept him in the spotlight between international assignments.

## Usman Tariq's Record: The Numbers Behind the Debate

The figures show a bowler who has taken wickets consistently. They also hold a small hint of why Shakeel's remark has drawn attention. These are the career numbers reported as of late September 2026:

- **T20Is:** nine matches, 18 wickets, an average of 11.16 and an economy rate of 6.66, with two four-wicket hauls and best figures of 4/16.
- **CPL 2026 (Trinbago Knight Riders):** nine matches, 10 wickets, an average of 21.30 and an economy rate of 6.26, with best figures of 3/21.

Tariq's economy rate stayed tight in both sets of figures. His bowling average, the number of runs conceded per wicket, is noticeably higher in the CPL than in his T20I career. The two are different competitions with small samples, so the gap does not prove anything on its own. It does show that he picked up wickets less cheaply in the Caribbean than for Pakistan, which fits the idea that batters are learning how to handle him.

## Why Batters Are Finding It Easier to Prepare

Tariq's action is unusual, and anything unusual can be studied and rehearsed. Reports describe a short, zigzag run-up, a brief pause in his delivery stride and a slinging, round-arm release. Once a batter has seen the rhythm repeatedly, the first-time confusion that helps a new bowler tends to reduce.

Teams have openly worked on this. Before the India versus Pakistan group match at the R. Premadasa Stadium in Colombo on 15 February 2026, India captain Suryakumar Yadav was reported to have copied Tariq's action in the nets so that India's batters could practise against it. A separate training clip from the tournament, which went viral, showed a net bowler pausing mid-stride before releasing the ball to imitate Tariq. Analysts quoted in the coverage said the change in rhythm can create hesitation in a batter's timing and judgement of length.

One analyst also noted that Tariq is slow through the air, which is a risk in T20 cricket, where batters are encouraged to attack. A bowler who relies on rhythm and deception has less margin once the rhythm is familiar.

## The Bowling Action Debate

Tariq's action has also been questioned by some commentators. He was reported for a suspect action in the Pakistan Super League and cleared after testing, though sources differ on whether this happened once or twice [VERIFY: number of PSL reports and the dates of clearance]. Coverage ahead of the World Cup also said his elbows are hypermobile, which stops him from fully straightening his arm, a trait that has been compared with Sri Lanka's Muttiah Muralitharan.

Former India off-spinner Ravichandran Ashwin said that the legality of an action can only be judged at an ICC-accredited testing centre, where the permitted limit for elbow extension is 15 degrees. He also said he believes the pause is legal because it is a consistent part of Tariq's regular action. Indian umpire Anil Chaudhary likewise described the delivery as fair. Australia's Cameron Green was seen mimicking the action in anger after being dismissed by Tariq in a series before the World Cup, and former England captain Kevin Pietersen was among those who questioned it.

The debate is separate from Shakeel's comments, but it helps explain why Tariq draws more scrutiny than most new spinners. Opponents, analysts and umpires have all spent time examining how he bowls.

## What Innovation Could Mean for Tariq

Shakeel did not say what Tariq should change, and none of the reports indicate that Tariq has altered his approach. Some context on his existing skills is available, though. Coverage during the World Cup noted that, besides his stock off-spin, he also bowls a googly and a leg-spinner and can make the ball drift away from the batter.

During Pakistan's match against Namibia in Colombo, a report in Arab News observed that his action never changed, but his pace and the type of delivery did. That points to where the room for adaptation may lie. A batter who has prepared for the pause and the run-up still has to read what comes out of the hand. In practical terms, "doing something different" for a bowler like Tariq is more likely to mean mixing up speeds, lengths and variations than changing the action itself. This is an analytical reading of the situation, not a claim made by Shakeel or by Tariq.
