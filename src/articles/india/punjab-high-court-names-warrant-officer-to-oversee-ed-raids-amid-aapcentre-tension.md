The Punjab and Haryana High Court has issued an exceptional procedural order appointing a court Warrant Officer to supervise ongoing search and seizure operations conducted by the federal Enforcement Directorate (ED) across multiple locations in Punjab.

The court's intervention followed an urgent petition filed by the Punjab state administration seeking judicial oversight to ensure that federal investigative agencies conduct ongoing searches in strict compliance with statutory guidelines, legal parameters, and procedural due process.

The Enforcement Directorate's operations have focused on multiple high-profile locations in the state. These include the offices of a Punjab government urban development body in Mohali, the premises of a senior administrative official under scrutiny for alleged irregularities in commercial building approvals, and the Ludhiana residence of a relative of Punjab Cabinet Minister Hardeep Singh Mundian.

The simultaneous raids ignited intense political controversy between the ruling Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) government in Punjab and the central government. State government officials and political leaders characterized the enforcement actions as politically motivated interference in state affairs, while central investigative authorities maintained that the searches were part of routine legal inquiries into financial irregularities and money laundering allegations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA).

Under the High Court’s directive, the appointed Warrant Officer is tasked with monitoring the execution of the search warrants on-site, verifying that procedural seizure memos are maintained in accordance with law, and preventing unauthorized actions. The Warrant Officer is required to submit a comprehensive compliance report directly to the High Court, providing a neutral judicial check during the ongoing investigative proceedings.
