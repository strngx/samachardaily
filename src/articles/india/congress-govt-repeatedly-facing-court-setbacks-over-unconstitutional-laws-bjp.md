The Himachal Pradesh High Court on Tuesday ruled that the state government's involvement in the appointment of vice‑chancellors to state universities violates constitutional provisions. The judgment nullified a set of notifications that had allowed the chief minister’s office to nominate candidates for the top academic posts, a move the court said infringed on the autonomy guaranteed to higher education institutions under the Indian Constitution. The decision came after a petition filed by the opposition Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) challenged the legality of the appointments, arguing that the executive branch had overstepped its authority.

BJP state president Jairam Thakur seized on the verdict, stating that it was another example of the Congress‑led government in Himachal Pradesh repeatedly facing setbacks in courts over laws he described as unconstitutional. Thakur cited earlier judgments that had struck down provisions related to land acquisition and anti‑encroachment measures, suggesting a pattern of legal defeats for the Sukhu administration. He urged the public to view the High Court’s ruling as evidence of the Congress’s disregard for constitutional limits.

Chief Minister Sukhvinder Singh Sukhu’s government, which came to power in December 2022, defended the appointments as necessary to ensure merit‑based leadership in the state’s universities. The administration has indicated it will appeal the decision, asserting that the notifications were issued after extensive consultations with academic bodies and were intended to streamline the selection process.

The legal tussle adds to a growing list of disputes between the state’s ruling party and the opposition, with both sides preparing for the next electoral cycle. The High Court’s order not only affects the immediate governance of higher‑education institutions but also sets a precedent that could influence how state governments across India draft and implement similar statutes.
