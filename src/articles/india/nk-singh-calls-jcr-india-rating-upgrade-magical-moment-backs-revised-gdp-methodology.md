Former Finance Commission chairman N K Singh has described Japan Credit Rating's (JCR) decision to upgrade India's sovereign credit rating as a 'magical moment', underscoring the country's improving macroeconomic standing under the current policy framework. Singh, who has held several senior positions in Indian economic governance, said the upgrade reflects growing international confidence in India's fiscal trajectory and growth prospects.

Speaking on the development, Singh also threw his weight behind India's revised GDP methodology, pushing back against critics who have questioned the government's recent growth projections. The Centre has earlier this year defended an estimated 7.8 percent expansion, dismissing opposition and independent economists who flagged concerns over the calculation method being used.

The dual endorsement from Singh comes at a time when India's macroeconomic narrative is being closely watched by global investors and rating agencies. With the economy being benchmarked against revised base-year parameters, official data has indicated stronger headline numbers than those projected under earlier computation models.

Singh's comments are notable given his stature as a veteran policymaker who chaired the 15th Finance Commission and has been associated with multiple rounds of reform-related consultations. His backing adds institutional weight to the government's position that the new methodology accurately captures structural shifts in services-led growth, formalisation, and digital transactions.

The debate over GDP computation has gained traction in recent weeks, with political leaders and economists exchanging barbs over whether the figures reflect ground-level economic activity. Singh's intervention is being read as an attempt to reset the conversation around data credibility and India's long-term growth story.
