The Delhi government has ordered the immediate sealing of all illegal five-storey buildings across the capital. According to an official announcement from the Chief Minister's Office (CMO), the directive is part of a wide-ranging safety crackdown launched in the wake of a deadly building collapse. The government has instructed municipal authorities to identify and seal non-compliant structures without delay, emphasizing that public safety will be the top priority.

The deadly collapse in a densely populated neighborhood of the capital exposed the severe risks of unchecked urban expansion. In the affected area, residents and local activists had long warned about the structural instability of aging buildings that were illegally extended upwards to accommodate growing populations. Despite repeated warnings, the lack of strict enforcement allowed property owners to construct additional floors without proper structural audits or fire safety clearances, ultimately culminating in the tragedy.

The CMO's directive specifically targets the Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD), tasking it with mapping all illegal five-storey structures and executing the sealing process. Officials stated that the crackdown will not tolerate any violations of municipal building bylaws. The drive aims to deter property owners from attempting illegal constructions and to enforce accountability on local officials who might turn a blind eye to such violations, ensuring that safety standards are strictly upheld.

While the sealing drive is expected to improve public safety, it also presents a massive logistical and humanitarian challenge. Thousands of families living in these illegally constructed units face the prospect of displacement. The government is currently working on a rehabilitation framework to ensure that tenants are not unfairly penalized, focusing instead on the builders and property owners who violated the law. Urban planning experts have welcomed the move, stating that Delhi's rapid and unregulated vertical growth has long outpaced its infrastructure and safety mechanisms.

The announcement has sparked a mixed reaction among local communities, with some praising the government's tough stance on safety, while others expressed concern over the potential housing shortage. As the municipal corporations begin their surveys, the government has promised a transparent and systematic approach to the sealing process. The deadly collapse has finally forced a long-overdue reckoning on the issue of illegal constructions in the capital, setting the stage for a major overhaul of urban safety regulations.
