Prime Minister Narendra Modi released a direct video message to the nation, celebrating India’s robust 7.8 percent economic growth recorded in the first quarter (Q1) while renewing his call for self-reliance and domestic spending. In his address, the Prime Minister framed the strong expansion figures as proof of India's resilient economic fundamentals, urging citizens to sustain momentum by prioritizing local goods and domestic service sectors.

Addressing the public in a selfie-style video format, PM Modi specifically cautioned against unnecessary expenditure on imported commodities, advising citizens to reconsider heavy spending on foreign gold. He highlighted how excessive gold imports place pressure on national reserves and trade balances, urging individuals to channel their savings into domestic assets and production sectors that actively support the home economy.

Expanding on his renewed swadeshi pitch, the Prime Minister also appealed to families to refrain from hosting lavish celebrations and weddings outside the country. By urging citizens not to marry abroad, Modi emphasized the immense economic value of retaining high-value ceremonial expenditure within domestic borders. He stressed that holding milestone events inside India directly supports local hospitality, artisan markets, and regional service industries.

The Prime Minister's message underscores an ongoing executive effort to align consumer behavior with broader macroeconomic policy. While the latest 7.8 percent quarterly growth figure demonstrates significant national momentum, government leadership remains focused on strengthening internal demand and stemming capital outflows tied to foreign luxury consumption and heavy import dependencies.

By linking strong GDP performance directly to everyday fiscal choices, Modi's address signals a reinforced emphasis on national self-reliance. The call to purchase local products and retain high-ticket spending within domestic borders comes as policymakers evaluate key growth drivers and seek to insulate the economy against external trade pressures.
