On 5 September 2026, schools, colleges and government institutions across India will observe Teacher’s Day, a national tribute to Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, the country’s second President and a distinguished philosopher‑educator. The date aligns with his birth anniversary, a fact that has been institutionalised since the early 1960s to honour his lifelong advocacy for the teaching profession.

The decision to celebrate Teacher’s Day on Radhakrishnan’s birthday was formalised by the Ministry of Education in the early 1960s, following a recommendation that his stature as a scholar and his belief that teachers are the true architects of a nation deserved a dedicated day of recognition. The move was intended to elevate the social standing of educators, encouraging students to view teachers as mentors and moral guides, much as Radhakrishnan himself had done throughout his academic career.

Central to the celebration is the ancient guru‑shishya (teacher‑disciple) tradition, a cornerstone of Indian cultural heritage that emphasizes respect, personal guidance and the transmission of knowledge across generations. This tradition underpins the modern observance, reminding citizens that the teacher‑student bond extends beyond the classroom to shape values, character and societal progress.

Teachers continue to play a pivotal role in India’s development agenda, driving literacy, skill acquisition and social mobility for millions. Government reports consistently cite educators as critical agents in achieving the nation’s educational targets, from universal primary education to higher‑skill vocational training. The annual observance therefore serves both as a symbolic salute and a practical reminder of the sector’s importance.

In today’s rapidly digitising landscape, the role of teachers is evolving. The pandemic‑induced shift to online learning, the rise of blended classrooms and the integration of artificial‑intelligence tools have expanded the teacher’s toolkit, demanding continuous professional development. Teacher’s Day 2026 is being used by educational bodies to spotlight these changes, encouraging policy dialogue on upskilling, digital infrastructure and the mental‑health support needed for educators navigating new pedagogical frontiers.
