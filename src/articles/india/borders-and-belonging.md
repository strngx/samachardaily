Singh explains that 'Rehmat' emerged from his long‑standing fascination with the porous borders that shape everyday life in Punjab. The film weaves fictional characters with real‑world testimonies, blurring the line between story‑telling and ethnographic observation to capture the region’s complex sense of belonging.

He says his return to Punjab is both artistic and emotional, a desire to document the evolving cultural landscape that he left as a young filmmaker. By employing a hybrid format, Singh aims to let the audience hear authentic voices while still delivering the cinematic arc of a feature film.

The director also reflects on the broader implications of border narratives, noting that they resonate beyond Punjab’s geography, touching on migration, identity, and the lingering impact of historical partitions. 'Rehmat' thus serves as a conduit for dialogue between the past and present, fiction and fact.
