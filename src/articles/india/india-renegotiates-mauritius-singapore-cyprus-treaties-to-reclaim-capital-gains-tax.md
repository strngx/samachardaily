India has moved to renegotiate its Double Tax Avoidance Agreements with Mauritius, Singapore and Cyprus, addressing historical bilateral frameworks that previously allowed foreign investors to claim residency-based exemptions on domestic capital gains taxes.

The amended treaty protocols align India’s international tax architecture with the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting Action Plan, specifically incorporating measures to prevent treaty abuse and enhance tax transparency. Under the revised terms, the bilateral arrangements transition from residence-based taxation to source-based taxation, granting India the sovereign right to levy capital gains tax on the alienation of shares in Indian companies.

To ensure stability for institutional investors and prevent market disruption, the amended agreements incorporate grandfathering provisions. These clauses safeguard investments in equity shares acquired prior to April 1, 2017, ensuring that historical holdings remain protected under earlier treaty rules.

The renegotiations reflect India’s broader statutory policy under Section 90 of the Income Tax Act, establishing equitable taxation rights on domestic capital appreciation while maintaining established foreign direct investment channels.
