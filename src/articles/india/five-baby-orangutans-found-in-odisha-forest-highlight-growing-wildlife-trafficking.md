On Sept 8, forest officials in Ranakantha, Balasore district, Odisha, discovered five infant orangutans inside a protected reserve, a rare occurrence far from the species’ natural habitat.

The discovery points to active wildlife‑trafficking networks operating along India’s eastern seaboard, where illegal trade persists despite recent high‑profile seizures.

Investigations indicate that South Indian states function both as sources and destinations for trafficked wildlife, with Chennai identified as a principal transit hub for such illicit movements.

Experts say the case underscores the need for stronger surveillance, advanced interception technology and wider public‑awareness campaigns to reinforce anti‑trafficking measures.
