A sudden 30-minute thunderstorm delivering approximately 37 mm of intense localized rainfall across Hyderabad’s Old City caused a section of lime-mortar plaster on the south-western facade of the historic Charminar to detach and fall to the ground near the Laad Bazaar pathway.

Security personnel stationed at the pedestrianized monument precinct secured the perimeter immediately following the detachment, confirming that no tourists or local market vendors sustained injuries. Officials from the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) Hyderabad Circle inspected the exposed masonry on Wednesday morning, identifying that the damaged patch involved secondary surface stucco rather than the primary load-bearing granite and limestone core.

Conservation engineers noted that the affected area, situated beneath the lower ornamental cornice of the southwest minaret, had been under routine monitoring for moisture seepage following consecutive heavy monsoon spells. The ASI confirmed that specialized conservation tenders have already been finalized to execute traditional lime, jaggery, and marble-dust mortar repairs in adherence to international heritage preservation standards.

The 16th-century monument, commissioned in 1591 by Sultan Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, undergoes periodic structural stability assessments, with preservation teams scheduling immediate scaffolding installation to stabilize adjacent decorative stucco mouldings.
