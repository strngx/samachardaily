On September 3, India’s High Commissioner to Kiribati, Suneet Mehta, met Kiribati’s Minister for Infrastructure and Sustainable Energy, Tekeeua Tarati, in South Tarawa. The two sides reiterated the “enduring partnership” between New Delhi and the Pacific island nation and outlined plans to expand cooperation across infrastructure, renewable energy and sustainable development, a statement the Indian High Commission in Fiji posted on X.

The dialogue builds on a broader India‑Pacific engagement that began with Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s 2023 Forum for India‑Pacific Islands Cooperation (FIPIC) initiative. As part of that programme, Mehta visited a newly‑opened dialysis centre in Kiribati, underscoring India’s commitment to health infrastructure in the region. The centre is one of several dialysis units pledged for all 14 Pacific Island Countries under the FIPIC health‑care drive.

In a separate meeting with Kiribati’s Health Minister Bootii Nauan, Mehta discussed expanding health collaboration. Key topics included the supply of affordable medicines, additional dialysis equipment, the introduction of sea‑ambulances for remote islands, and capacity‑building programmes for local medical staff. Both governments said the aim is to create a healthier Pacific by leveraging Indian expertise and technology.

The day also saw the launch of a Virgin Coconut Oil (VCO) project in Tarawa, hailed by the Indian High Commission as a “significant milestone” in bilateral ties. President Taneti Maamau, Minister for Tourism, Commerce, Industry and Cooperatives Tangariki Reete, and several other cabinet members attended the ceremony. Officials highlighted the project’s potential to boost local livelihoods, add value to Kiribati’s coconut harvests and open new export avenues.

Beyond health and agriculture, Mehta and Kiribati’s Secretary of Foreign Affairs and Immigration, Tearinaki Tanielu, explored deeper cooperation in renewable‑energy infrastructure, such as solar and wind installations, and broader sustainable‑development goals. Both sides emphasized a shared vision of a resilient “Blue Pacific” and pledged to translate discussions into concrete projects over the coming months.
