A large group of activists from three Punjab-based frontline organisations descended on Bathinda on Saturday, attempting to reach Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann's event at Shaheed Bhagat Singh Stadium. The protesters, drawn from the Democratic Teachers' Front, the 1158 Assistant Professors and Librarians Front Punjab and the Employees and Pensioners Joint Front, organised a march that grew confrontational as it neared the venue where the Chief Minister's programme was underway.

According to the Tribune India report, the demonstrators broke through barricades at two separate points along the security perimeter, forcing police to escalate their response. Officers deployed in the area resorted to a mild lathicharge to push back the crowds and prevent the protest from spilling into the Chief Minister's event space. Details on injuries, detentions or specific demands of the three organisations were not available in the source dispatch.

The choice of venue — Shaheed Bhagat Singh Stadium in Bathinda, the Chief Minister's home district and a politically significant Aam Aadmi Party stronghold in the Malwa region — underlines the protest organisers' calculation that maximum visibility near a high-profile Mann event would amplify their cause. Punjab has witnessed recurring confrontations between the Mann government and employee-teacher unions over recruitment, regularisation and service-condition issues.

Saturday's breach is the latest in a string of public-order confrontations around Mann's public appearances, and comes at a time when the AAP-led state government is already engaged in back-channel talks with several employee federations. The use of lathicharge, even if described as 'mild', is likely to harden positions on both sides, with the aggrieved unions accusing the administration of heavy-handedness while police maintain that the response was proportionate to a barricade breach.

The Tribune India report did not include a statement from Chief Minister Mann's office, the Punjab Police leadership or any of the three protesting organisations beyond the initial account of the breach and the lathicharge. Bathinda police's version of events and any subsequent FIRs are expected to clarify whether organisers face charges of unlawful assembly, obstructing a public servant or damage to public property.
