The Kerala government announced plans to explore the creation of a dedicated Tourism and Hospitality University, a move aimed at strengthening the state’s human‑resource base for its thriving travel sector. Officials described the proposal as a strategic response to the growing demand for skilled professionals in hotels, resorts, travel agencies and related services across the state.

Kerala, often billed as “God’s Own Country,” has long relied on its natural beauty, cultural heritage and culinary reputation to attract domestic and international visitors. While the sector contributes significantly to the state’s economy, industry leaders have highlighted a persistent gap between the volume of tourists and the availability of locally trained staff equipped with modern service standards and management expertise.

The proposed university would focus on a curriculum that blends theoretical instruction with practical training, leveraging existing hospitality hubs and tourism hotspots for on‑site learning. Planners envision partnerships with established hotels, travel operators and international tourism bodies to ensure that graduates are job‑ready and can meet global service benchmarks.

Stakeholders, including the Kerala Tourism Department and leading educational institutions, are expected to conduct feasibility studies, assess funding models and outline governance structures over the coming months. The initiative is being framed as a long‑term investment that could generate new employment opportunities, encourage entrepreneurship in eco‑tourism and support the state’s ambition to become a benchmark destination for sustainable travel.

If approved, the university could also serve as a research centre, generating data on visitor trends, digital marketing strategies and climate‑responsive tourism practices. Such insights would help policymakers fine‑tune promotional campaigns and infrastructure projects, reinforcing Kerala’s competitive edge in the increasingly crowded Indian tourism market.
