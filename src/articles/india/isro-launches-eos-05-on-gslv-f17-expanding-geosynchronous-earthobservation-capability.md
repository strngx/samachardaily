In the pre‑dawn hours of September 4, 2026, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) lifted off from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre at Sriharikota, firing the Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle‑F17 (GSLV‑F17). The rocket carried a single payload – the EOS‑05 satellite – weighing 2,367 kilograms, marking another milestone in India’s heavy‑lift launch programme.

EOS‑05 is a sophisticated Earth‑observation spacecraft designed to operate in a geosynchronous orbit, a position that allows it to remain fixed relative to a point on the Earth’s surface. This orbital slot enables continuous monitoring of a specific region, delivering high‑resolution imagery and data for applications ranging from agriculture to disaster management.

The successful insertion of EOS‑05 into geosynchronous orbit underscores ISRO’s growing proficiency in delivering high‑value remote‑sensing assets. By maintaining a persistent view of the same terrain, the satellite can provide near‑real‑time updates on crop health, water resources, and urban expansion, thereby supporting both governmental planning and private‑sector analytics.

The GSLV‑F17 launch follows a series of recent missions that have demonstrated ISRO’s ability to place heavy payloads into high‑energy orbits. Each successful flight reinforces confidence among international partners and commercial customers that India can reliably deliver complex satellites to demanding orbital regimes.

With EOS‑05 now in its operational slot, ISRO will begin a series of on‑orbit tests to validate the satellite’s imaging payload and data transmission systems. Once calibrated, the satellite’s data stream will be integrated into the nation’s remote‑sensing network, enhancing the granularity and timeliness of information available to policymakers, researchers, and industry stakeholders.
