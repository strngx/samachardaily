A resident of Manipur who survived a violent ambush on a highway last week told investigators that the assailants were dressed in camouflage uniforms, a detail that could help authorities narrow down the perpetrators. The survivor, who asked to remain unnamed for safety reasons, said the attack occurred near the town of Churachandpur around dusk, when a group of five gunmen opened fire on his vehicle, forcing it to stop. He described the uniforms as resembling those worn by the Indian army and paramilitary forces, though the attackers did not display any insignia or identification badges. According to the survivor, the gunmen used automatic rifles and a hand‑grenade before fleeing into the surrounding forest. He managed to escape with minor injuries and later reported the incident to local police, who have now opened a case and are reviewing CCTV footage from nearby checkpoints. The police spokesperson confirmed that the investigation is ongoing and that the camouflage clothing is a key lead, but emphasized that wearing such attire does not automatically implicate any particular force.

The incident adds to a series of violent encounters reported in the northeastern state over the past months, where civilians and security personnel have been targeted in remote areas. While the exact motive behind the ambush remains unclear, officials are exploring possibilities ranging from insurgent activity to intra‑tribal disputes. The survivor's testimony is being cross‑checked with other eyewitness accounts and with patrol logs to determine whether the attackers were irregular militants attempting to blend in or members of an organized outfit using military‑style gear to sow confusion.
