The Kerala High Court has conducted hearings in the case of E.T. Firoz vs. Recovery Officer, Debts Recovery Tribunal-I and Others, examining legal challenges raised against enforcement and property attachment actions taken by debt recovery authorities in Ernakulam.

The writ petition was filed by petitioner E.T. Firoz contesting specific procedural orders and recovery measures executed by the Recovery Officer attached to the Debts Recovery Tribunal-I (DRT-I). The petitioner alleged that the administrative recovery proceedings failed to adhere to statutory procedural guidelines and natural justice principles, warranting constitutional intervention under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.

The legal proceedings center on the statutory framework established by the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 (RDB Act). Under this framework, once a Debts Recovery Tribunal issues a recovery certificate in favor of a financial creditor, the designated Recovery Officer possesses specialized powers to execute the decree through property attachment, asset auctions, or appointment of receivers.

During the hearings, the High Court evaluated the relationship between the extraordinary writ jurisdiction of High Courts and the statutory appeal mechanisms available under Section 30 of the RDB Act, which allows aggrieved parties to appeal a Recovery Officer’s decisions directly to the Presiding Officer of the Debts Recovery Tribunal. Counsel for the financial creditors maintained that statutory appellate remedies must be exhausted before invoking constitutional remedies.

The High Court heard arguments from both sides regarding the legality of the Recovery Officer's procedural notices and reserved further directions. The case highlights ongoing judicial scrutiny over debt enforcement mechanisms, ensuring that recovery tribunals balance prompt creditor claims with statutory debtor protections.
