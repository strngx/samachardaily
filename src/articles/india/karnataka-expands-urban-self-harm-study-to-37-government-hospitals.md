The Karnataka health ministry disclosed on Monday that the Urban Self‑Harm Study, a research initiative focused on tracking self‑inflicted injuries in city environments, will now operate in 37 government hospitals throughout the state. The expansion follows an initial pilot that covered a limited number of facilities and is intended to generate a more comprehensive picture of self‑harm trends among urban residents.

Self‑harm, encompassing both non‑fatal attempts and suicidal behavior, has emerged as a growing public‑health concern in India's rapidly expanding cities. While national surveys have highlighted rising incidence, reliable data at the district and hospital level remain scarce. The study seeks to standardise reporting protocols, capture demographic and clinical details, and identify common precipitating factors, thereby filling a critical evidence gap for policymakers and clinicians.

Participating hospitals range from district medical colleges in Bengaluru and Mysuru to smaller community health centres in Hubli‑Dharwad and Bellary. Each facility will appoint a dedicated data‑collection officer to record cases admitted for self‑injury, document treatment pathways, and feed anonymised information into a central state database managed by the Department of Health and Family Welfare.

Officials say the broadened network will enable real‑time monitoring of self‑harm spikes, facilitate early intervention strategies, and support the design of targeted mental‑health programmes. The data will also be shared with the National Institute of Mental Health and Neurosciences (NIMHANS) for advanced analysis and to inform national guidelines on suicide prevention.

The rollout is scheduled to begin in the first week of October, with periodic audits to ensure data quality. Health administrators have urged medical staff to receive brief training on the study's protocols and to maintain strict confidentiality for patients, emphasizing that the initiative is a preventive tool rather than a punitive measure.
