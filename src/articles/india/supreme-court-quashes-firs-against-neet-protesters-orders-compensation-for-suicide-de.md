The Supreme Court has quashed all FIRs registered against protesters who took to the streets during the nationwide agitation over the NEET-UG examination in July, marking a decisive judicial closure to one of the most volatile student movements India has seen in recent years. A bench also directed that compensation be paid to the families of protesters who died by suicide during the course of the agitation, acknowledging the human cost of the episode that had paralyzed parts of Delhi and prompted solidarity rallies in several other cities.

The protests erupted in July after allegations of paper leaks and irregularities in the conduct of the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (Undergraduate), the single largest medical entrance examination in India administered by the National Testing Agency (NTA). What began as localised demonstrations by aspirants and their parents in cities including Delhi, Jaipur, and Patna quickly snowballed into a nationwide movement, with allegations of suicides linked to examination pressure surfacing alongside demands for a fresh, leak-free test.

During the weeks of unrest, Delhi Police registered multiple first information reports against demonstrators, citing violations of public order, obstruction of traffic, and damage to public property. Several students were briefly detained, and the crackdown drew sharp criticism from opposition parties, student unions, and civil society groups who argued that the response was disproportionate to a peaceful expression of grievance. The matter eventually reached the Supreme Court through a cluster of petitions seeking protection for the protesters and the quashing of what petitioners termed 'vindictive' criminal cases.

By ordering the FIRs quashed, the apex court has effectively ruled that the registration of criminal cases in the circumstances disclosed was not warranted, though the precise reasoning of the bench will be available once the full text of the judgment is released. The direction on compensation signals the Court's recognition that the families of those who took their own lives during the agitation are entitled to relief, and may set a precedent for how the state responds to mass student distress in future examination-related crises.

The ruling comes against the backdrop of continued scrutiny of the NTA's functioning, with Parliament having debated the conduct of competitive examinations and the government announcing several structural reforms to examination governance earlier in the year. For lakhs of NEET-UG aspirants and their families, the Court's intervention is expected to bring at least partial closure to a turbulent chapter, even as questions over systemic reforms in the conduct of high-stakes entrance tests remain unresolved.
