The Election Commission of India faced heightened public and procedural scrutiny following reports that two Election Commissioners formally recorded objections on multiple occasions regarding aspects of the nationwide Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, prompting the constitutional body to publicly clarify its decision-making processes in late September 2026.

According to investigative reporting by The Indian Express, Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi recorded written observations and objections at least 14 times over a ten-month period. The internal notes specifically questioned procedural mechanisms governing the addition and deletion of voters, the verification standards required for removing entries, and access controls surrounding the centralized digital electoral-roll database. The Special Intensive Revision, which commenced in mid-2025 across 30 States and Union Territories, resulted in the removal of over 13 crore deceased, shifted, or duplicate voter entries from draft rolls to improve voter register accuracy.

In response to reports of internal division, the Election Commission of India issued formal press communiques on September 24 and September 26, 2026, explicitly rejecting assertions of an institutional rift. The full Commission stated that individual observations, differing viewpoints, and detailed written notes are standard components of healthy collegial deliberation within a multi-member constitutional body. The ECI affirmed that all formal statutory decisions regarding the Special Intensive Revision were adopted with the unanimous approval of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and both Election Commissioners. The Commission further clarified that correspondence regarding the administrative deputation of officers did not reflect policy disagreements over electoral roll administration.

The procedural scrutiny follows a May 2026 ruling by the Supreme Court of India, which upheld the constitutional legitimacy of the Special Intensive Revision under Article 324 and the Representation of the People Act, confirming the Commission's authority to clean voter registers. Following the internal reviews, the Commission introduced administrative refinements in late September 2026, extending objection filing periods in states including Maharashtra and Delhi while streamlining documentation requirements for unmapped electors.
