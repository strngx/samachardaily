Canada’s Minister of International Trade stated that negotiations for a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) with India are progressing steadily, expressing confidence that bilateral talks can conclude before the end of the year, according to a report by Reuters.

Speaking during trade deliberations, the minister noted that both nations are working through priority market access chapters covering agricultural commodities, clean technology, critical minerals, and professional service mobility. The agreement aims to establish preferential tariff rates, streamline customs procedures, and provide transparent regulatory frameworks for cross-border corporate investments.

Bilateral negotiations had previously focused on an Early Progress Trade Agreement (EPTA) as an interim step before expanding into the full comprehensive treaty. Key Canadian export interests center on agricultural goods including pulses, potash fertilizers, and renewable energy equipment, while Indian exporters seek expanded access for pharmaceutical products, textiles, automotive components, and information technology services.

Trade economists emphasize that concluding the pact will diversify bilateral supply chains and provide structured dispute resolution mechanisms for institutional investors deploying capital across infrastructure and energy projects in both countries.
