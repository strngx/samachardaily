Representatives of Meta India arrived at the headquarters of the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) on Wednesday to respond to a formal summons concerning a probe into advertisements flagged under the Child Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Monitoring (CSEAM) framework.

The NCPCR, an autonomous statutory body tasked with safeguarding children's rights across India, initiated the inquiry after receiving complaints that certain ads on Meta's platforms may have breached child‑protection guidelines. The commission’s notice required Meta to provide details on the nature of the ads, the targeting parameters used, and the mechanisms in place to detect and remove harmful content.

Meta, which operates Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and other services in India, has previously faced regulatory pressure over content moderation and data privacy. In a brief statement, the company said it "cooperates fully with Indian authorities and is committed to ensuring a safe online environment for children," adding that it has robust AI‑driven tools to identify and block prohibited material.

Legal experts note that the NCPCR’s jurisdiction extends to any digital content that could potentially endanger minors, and that the commission can recommend corrective actions, fines, or even refer matters to the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology for further action. The outcome of this particular hearing could set a precedent for how social‑media giants handle child‑safety compliance in India.

The hearing, which is part of a broader wave of regulatory scrutiny on tech platforms, follows recent amendments to the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics) Rules, which impose stricter obligations on intermediaries to monitor and report child‑related exploitation. Observers will be watching for any directives the NCPCR may issue to Meta India in the coming days.
