A deep depression moving along the Andhra-Odisha coastline brought heavy rains to both states, prompting red warning alerts and large-scale disaster response deployment — while a viral social media claim about a non-existent "Cyclone Arnab" spread simultaneously.

## What the IMD said

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) issued official red warnings for four districts in Odisha: **Malkangiri, Koraput, Rayagada, and Gajapati**. In press bulletins published through its official portal, IMD classified the system as an intense deep depression in the northwest Bay of Bengal. The system was tracking toward the coast and was expected to bring very heavy to extremely heavy rainfall to the affected areas.

IMD explicitly stated in its communications that there was no cyclone by the name "Arnab" and that the viral social media posts were unfounded. The Hindu's reporting corroborated the IMD's official classification and the misinformation context.

## Response on the ground

Authorities deployed National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) units across the red-alerted districts. Tens of thousands of residents were evacuated from coastal and low-lying areas ahead of expected storm surges and river flooding. Cities including Kolkata prepared separately for heavy rainfall as the system continued its eastward track.

## Why the misinformation matters

"Cyclone Arnab" did not exist. The viral label — apparently coined on social media with no meteorological basis — risked diverting public attention from genuine safety warnings. The episode is a recurring feature of severe weather events in India, where unofficial storm names circulate widely on social media before IMD makes formal cyclone designations. IMD naming authority follows a defined Bay of Bengal rotational list; "Arnab" was not on it.
