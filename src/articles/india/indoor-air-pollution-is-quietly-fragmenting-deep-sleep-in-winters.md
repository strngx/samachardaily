Every winter, millions of people shut their bedroom windows against the haze, hoping four walls will shield them from polluted air and protect their own rest.

A fresh set of research from Delhi and other cities worldwide found that dirty air can move indoors, interrupting deep sleep without waking people.

The findings now suggest indoor air can disturb rest, adding to a sleep-deprived world where winter haze may interrupt deep sleep despite closed windows. The report points to indoor air as a quiet pathway of sleep loss during the winter.
