POCO announced the Indian launch of two mid‑range smartphones, the X8 and the X8 Power, on Thursday. Both devices are built around Qualcomm Snapdragon processors and are positioned as performance‑focused options for price‑sensitive consumers. The company highlighted a 120Hz AMOLED display for each model, promising smoother scrolling and richer colour reproduction compared with standard LCD panels.

The standout specification for the X8 Power is its 10,000mAh battery, a capacity that POCO says will comfortably support a full day of heavy usage, including gaming and video streaming. The standard X8 carries a slightly smaller battery, though the exact figure was not disclosed. Both phones support fast‑charging technology, enabling rapid top‑ups despite the large energy reserves.

In terms of design, POCO confirmed that the X8 and X8 Power will be offered in multiple colour finishes, though the precise palette was not enumerated in the release. Pricing tiers have been set for each variant, with the X8 Power positioned at a premium over the base X8 due to its larger battery and enhanced cooling system. The launch will be synchronized across POCO’s online store and authorized retail partners, ensuring nationwide availability shortly after the announced sale date.

The rollout comes as the Indian smartphone market continues to see intense competition among Chinese and domestic brands vying for market share in the sub‑₹20,000 segment. POCO, a sub‑brand of Xiaomi, has previously leveraged aggressive pricing and feature‑rich devices to capture a youthful demographic. By integrating a high‑refresh AMOLED panel and a flagship‑class battery, the X8 series aims to differentiate itself from rivals that often compromise on display quality or endurance.

Analysts note that the inclusion of Snapdragon processors aligns the X8 line with broader industry trends toward unified chipset ecosystems, which can simplify software updates and improve overall performance stability. POCO’s decision to bundle these specifications at a competitive price point could pressure competitors to accelerate their own feature upgrades, potentially reshaping the value proposition landscape in India’s mid‑range segment.
