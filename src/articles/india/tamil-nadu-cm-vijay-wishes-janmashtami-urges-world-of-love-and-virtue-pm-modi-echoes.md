On Friday, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Vijay took to the social‑media platform X to extend heartfelt Janmashtami greetings. In his post he invoked the teachings of Lord Krishna, stating that “righteousness will triumph over injustice, one must unflinchingly perform one’s duty in any circumstance, and life’s journey must be undertaken with mental fortitude and faith along the path of dharma.” He added that these principles can help create “a good world where love and virtue flourish alongside noble principles of living.”

Prime Minister Narendra Modi also marked the occasion on X, offering his own wishes to the nation. He highlighted the “divine messages of Lord Shri Krishna” as a source of “selfless action” that has historically guided humanity. Modi’s message called for the festival to “infuse positive energy and strength into everyone’s life,” framing Janmashtami as a catalyst for renewed vigor and prosperity across India.

Janmashtami, also known as Gokulashtami, commemorates the birth of Lord Krishna, traditionally believed to have occurred at midnight in Mathura. The festival, observed on Friday, sees devotees across the country engage in fasting, prayers, bhajans, and midnight temple visits. Iconic celebrations are centered in Mathura and Vrindavan, yet homes and temples nationwide decorate Krishna idols, prepare festive offerings, and perform ritual chants, underscoring the holiday’s deep cultural resonance.

The day’s observances extend beyond prayer. In many regions, especially in Maharashtra, the Dahi Handi tradition—where teams form human pyramids to break hanging pots of curd—takes place on September 5, marking the 2026 Dahi Handi celebration. Authorities typically deploy additional police and medical teams to manage crowds, while local businesses experience a surge in sales of sweets, dairy products, and decorative items, reflecting the festival’s economic ripple effect.
