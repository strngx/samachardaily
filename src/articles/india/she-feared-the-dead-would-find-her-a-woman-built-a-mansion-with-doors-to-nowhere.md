Sarah Lockwood Pardee Winchester, the widow of firearm magnate William Winchester, inherited a vast fortune in 1881 after his sudden death. Rather than retreat into conventional mourning, she moved to a modest farmhouse in San Jose, California, and embarked on an unprecedented construction project that would last nearly 40 years. Each year, new rooms, corridors, and architectural oddities were added, resulting in a 160-room labyrinth that defied logic. The mansion features doors that open onto solid walls, staircases that ascend to ceilings, and windows that overlook other rooms, creating a disorienting maze.

The prevailing legend holds that Sarah believed the spirits of those killed by Winchester rifles were seeking vengeance. To evade these restless souls, she supposedly kept the house in perpetual construction, thinking that constant building would confuse and placate the ghosts. While historians debate the veracity of this superstition, contemporary accounts confirm that Sarah hired dozens of carpenters and architects, often altering plans mid‑project. The house’s exterior grew into a sprawling Victorian estate, while the interior became a surreal landscape of secret passageways, double‑backed hallways and a famed “door to nowhere.”

When Sarah Winchester died in 1922, the mansion stood unfinished, a testament to her obsessive building spree. Her heirs sold the property, and it eventually opened to the public in the 1930s. Today, the Winchester Mystery House draws over a million visitors annually, who tour its enigmatic rooms and hear tales of hauntings, making it one of California’s most iconic tourist attractions.
