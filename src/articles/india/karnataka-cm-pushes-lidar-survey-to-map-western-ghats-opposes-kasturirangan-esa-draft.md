Karnataka Chief Minister Siddaramaiah has formally rejected the Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change’s (MoEFCC) draft notification designating ecologically sensitive areas (ESA) across the Western Ghats based on the Kasturirangan Committee report, advocating instead for high-resolution ground-level LiDAR mapping surveys, according to reporting by The Economic Times.

The state cabinet’s opposition centers on concerns that the central government's satellite-based delineation includes thousands of populated agricultural villages, coffee plantations, and rural settlements within proposed conservation zones, severely restricting local livelihoods and infrastructure development across ten districts, including Kodagu, Chikkamagaluru, Hassan, and Uttara Kannada.

Chief Minister Siddaramaiah proposed deploying aerial Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) technology combined with comprehensive plot-level cadastral ground surveys to accurately differentiate between fragile natural forest canopies and human-inhabited agricultural valleys. The state government argued that satellite imagery alone lacks the vertical resolution to map ground realities without causing undue hardship to rural farming communities.

Environmental conservationists, however, have cautioned against diluting conservation mandates, pointing to recent catastrophic landslides and soil erosion events in Wayanad and the coastal Ghats as stark evidence of accelerating ecological fragility. The central environment ministry has indicated that state recommendations will be evaluated before finalizing the statutory ESA boundary notification.
