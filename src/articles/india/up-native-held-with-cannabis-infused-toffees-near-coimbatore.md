The Tamil Nadu police arrested a man identified as a resident of Uttar Pradesh after seizing 10.8 kg of toffees laced with cannabis from a vehicle parked near Coimbatore’s industrial belt. The batch, valued at roughly ₹1 lakh, was discovered during a routine check by the city’s drug‑control unit, prompting immediate detention under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act.

Under India’s NDPS Act, possession of cannabis in any form can attract rigorous imprisonment of up to ten years and a fine. The law treats cannabis‑infused edibles the same as raw plant material, reflecting the government’s stance that any psycho‑active preparation is a controlled substance. Legal experts note that the quantity seized—over ten kilograms—places the case in the “commercial” category, which carries the harshest penalties.

Coimbatore, a major logistics hub in southern India, has increasingly featured in drug‑trafficking investigations, often serving as a transit point for narcotics moving from the north to ports on the western coast. Smugglers have turned to confectionery items such as toffees to mask the drug’s odor and evade detection, exploiting the city’s dense network of small‑scale manufacturers and transporters. This method mirrors recent seizures in other states where edible forms of cannabis have been used to conceal large volumes of the drug.

Senior Superintendent of Police (Crime) R. Mohan disclosed that the arrested individual is being interrogated for possible links to a larger syndicate operating across state borders. The police have lodged a formal charge sheet and forwarded the evidence to the district court, where the case will be tried under the NDPS provisions. Officials emphasized that the seizure underscores the need for heightened vigilance at checkpoints and among local businesses that could unwittingly become conduits for illicit substances.

The incident adds to a growing pattern of cannabis‑related busts across India, where law‑enforcement agencies report a steady rise in both raw plant and processed product seizures. Analysts attribute this trend to expanding domestic demand and the ease of transporting compact, edible forms. While the Uttar Pradesh suspect faces a serious legal battle, the case also highlights the inter‑state nature of drug networks that exploit India’s internal trade routes to move contraband from production zones to consumption markets.
