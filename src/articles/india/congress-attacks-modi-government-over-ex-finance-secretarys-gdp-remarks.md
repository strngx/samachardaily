The Congress launched a sharp political attack on the Modi government on Wednesday, seizing on reported remarks by former finance secretary Subhash Chandra Garg to question the credibility of India's GDP numbers under the present dispensation. The opposition party alleged that favourable growth headlines had been used as a substitute for genuine economic improvement, contending that optics cannot mask ground realities for households and businesses across the country.

Congress general secretary in-charge of communications Jairam Ramesh shared a video clip of Garg on his official social media handle, framing the former finance secretary's comments as vindication of the party's long-running charge that official growth statistics have been overstated. The Congress leadership quoted Garg to argue that the government had leaned on favourable methodologies and selective data presentation to project an image of economic strength that did not match the lived experience of ordinary Indians.

At the heart of the Congress critique is the contention that headline GDP figures, however polished, cannot alter the underlying condition of the economy. The party pointed to specific indicators of stress — including sluggish private investment, weak consumption in rural areas, and persistent unemployment — to argue that India was experiencing what it called a 'PR-driven recovery' rather than a broad-based expansion. By invoking a former finance secretary from within the institutional establishment, the Congress sought to widen the debate over data integrity beyond partisan lines and bring technocratic weight to its criticism.

The political confrontation over economic data has been a recurring flashpoint between the ruling BJP and the Congress, with opposition leaders repeatedly alleging that the back-series revisions carried out by the National Statistical Office inflated pre-pandemic growth rates and altered the political legacy of the previous UPA government. The BJP has consistently defended the methodology, asserting that India's statistical framework complies with international standards and that the economy has outperformed most major economies since the pandemic. The fresh Congress offensive, anchored this time in Garg's reported remarks, is likely to intensify scrutiny of upcoming quarterly GDP releases and the forthcoming full-year growth estimates, with both parties preparing to weaponise the numbers ahead of further electoral battles.
