A multi‑storey structure in Delhi's Satya Niketan neighbourhood collapsed early this week, killing six people and prompting an urgent search and rescue operation. The incident, reported by local media, has drawn immediate attention from municipal authorities and emergency services as they grapple with the scale of the disaster.

Rescue crews from the Delhi Fire Service, the National Disaster Response Force and private contractors arrived on the scene to begin clearing the heavy debris that has buried the building's lower floors. Teams equipped with hydraulic cutters, concrete breakers and trained canines are methodically sifting through the rubble, hoping to locate any individuals who may still be trapped beneath the collapsed sections.

The operation is being conducted under challenging conditions, with unstable remnants of the structure posing a constant risk to responders. Officials have set up a command centre nearby to coordinate efforts, monitor air quality, and ensure that safety protocols are observed as workers remove concrete slabs and steel beams. Medical personnel are on standby to provide immediate care to any survivors rescued from the site.

As the search progresses, the death toll has risen to six after additional bodies were recovered from the wreckage. Families of the missing have gathered outside the site, awaiting news while authorities continue to issue regular updates. The ongoing effort underscores the difficulty of locating victims in densely packed urban collapses, where each layer of debris can conceal further casualties.

Delhi's municipal administration has pledged a thorough investigation into the causes of the collapse, citing the need to examine construction compliance, structural integrity and any potential regulatory lapses. While no official findings have been released, officials have indicated that a detailed report will be compiled once the rescue phase concludes and the site is deemed safe for forensic examination.
