On Monday, the Bombay High Court entertained a petition filed by Satish Salian, father of the late Disha Salian, seeking the registration of a fresh First Information Report (FIR) and a new investigation into his daughter’s death. Disha, a 23‑year‑old software professional, was found dead in a Mumbai flat in March 2024, a case that quickly attracted public scrutiny and allegations of a botched police inquiry. Salian’s plea argues that the existing magisterial enquiry under Section 174 of the Criminal Procedure Code has been unduly prolonged and has failed to uncover the truth, warranting a fresh criminal probe. The bench, comprising senior judges, expressed concern over the duration of the Section 174 investigation, noting that such inquiries are meant to be time‑bound and that the delay could compromise evidence.

Aaditya Thackeray’s counsel, appearing for the Shiv Sena‑led state government, objected to the petition, describing it as a “politically motivated” attempt to derail the ongoing inquiry. The lawyer further insinuated that Satish Salian was being used as a pawn by political rivals seeking to score points against the ruling coalition. He urged the court to reject the demand for a new FIR, emphasizing that the existing investigation was sufficient and that reopening the case could set a precedent for litigants to weaponise criminal law for political gain. The courtroom witnessed a tense exchange as the judges weighed the merits of the petition against the broader implications for procedural fairness.
