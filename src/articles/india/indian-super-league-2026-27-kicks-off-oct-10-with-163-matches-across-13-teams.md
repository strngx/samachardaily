The Indian Super League (ISL) announced that its 2026-27 season will commence on October 10. The opening marks the start of a tightly packed calendar that will see clubs compete across eight months, culminating in a decisive league table that determines the season’s champion.

Thirteen teams will contest the season in a pure round‑robin format, meaning each club faces every other side both home and away. This structure generates a total of 163 matches, a schedule designed to provide consistent competition and maintain fan engagement throughout the extended campaign.

At the conclusion of the round‑robin phase, the team that finishes atop the league table will be declared the Shield Winners. This title is recognised as the official ISL championship, and the victorious club will also secure a berth in continental Asian competition, linking domestic success with broader regional exposure.

The eight‑month timeline gives clubs ample opportunity to manage player workloads, integrate new signings, and adapt tactics as the season unfolds. For supporters, the spread of fixtures promises regular matchday experiences, while broadcasters benefit from a sustained flow of content across the Indian football calendar.

Stakeholders across the league are now focusing on finalising squad registrations, arranging pre‑season training camps, and preparing marketing campaigns ahead of the October launch. The schedule’s length and the promise of an Asian competition slot add competitive pressure, encouraging clubs to invest in performance and infrastructure ahead of the new campaign.
