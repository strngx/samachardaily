Former Bigg Boss Marathi reality show contestant Irina Rudakova has approached the Mumbai police seeking intervention after a man allegedly assaulted her auto-rickshaw driver on a city road. According to the initial complaint, the unidentified man pulled the driver by the collar and verbally abused him during a confrontation, prompting Rudakova to step in and report the matter to law enforcement authorities.

The episode, which reportedly unfolded while Rudakova was travelling in the auto-rickshaw, has highlighted growing concerns around the safety of gig and informal transport workers in Mumbai. Auto-rickshaw drivers in the metropolis have long flagged vulnerability to road-rage incidents, fare disputes and physical altercations, particularly during peak traffic hours when tensions between motorists, pedestrians and three-wheeler operators frequently escalate. Rudakova, who gained popularity through her appearance on the Marathi edition of the long-running Bigg Boss franchise, used her public platform to escalate the matter rather than allow it to pass without action.

Mumbai's police machinery routinely registers non-cognizable complaints and FIRs relating to road altercations under provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, including sections dealing with voluntarily causing hurt, criminal intimidation and obscene or abusive language in public. Officials are expected to examine available evidence, including any footage that may have surfaced from bystanders or CCTV networks along the route, before proceeding with formal action against the accused. The identity of the assailant has not yet been disclosed publicly.

News of the complaint circulated widely on social media, with users expressing support for both Rudakova and the driver, while questioning why such incidents remain routine in urban India. Several commentators noted that bystanders often hesitate to intervene in street altercations for fear of legal entanglement or retaliation, making Rudakova's decision to formally lodge a complaint unusual in the public eye. Her post about seeking assistance went viral before being amplified by entertainment and news outlets.

The development comes at a time when reality television personalities in India have increasingly used their social media reach to draw attention to civic grievances, from harassment complaints to infrastructure failures. Police have stated that they are verifying the sequence of events described in Rudakova's complaint and will take appropriate steps once the inquiry is complete. No injuries requiring medical attention were reported in the immediate aftermath of the altercation.
