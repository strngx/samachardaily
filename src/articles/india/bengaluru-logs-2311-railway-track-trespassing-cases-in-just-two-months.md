Railway authorities in Bengaluru have flagged a sharp rise in track trespassing, recording 2,311 cases over the past two months. The figures underscore a persistent safety challenge on the city's busy suburban rail network, where pedestrian movement across and along active tracks continues to expose commuters to fatal risk. Officials confirmed that the cases have been logged as part of routine enforcement and surveillance across the Bengaluru division.

In response to the surge, the Railways has stepped up enforcement measures aimed at deterring trespassers, while simultaneously running awareness campaigns targeting both daily passengers and the wider public. The dual-track approach combines punitive action against those caught on the tracks with outreach designed to communicate the dangers and legal consequences of entering railway property without authorisation. Divisional officials are coordinating with station staff and local police to identify hotspots where trespassing incidents are most frequently reported.

Track trespassing remains one of the leading causes of accidental deaths on Indian Railways, and Bengaluru's urban rail corridors are particularly vulnerable given the high density of stations, level crossings and informal pedestrian pathways running alongside tracks. Authorities have repeatedly stressed that the practice carries not only the risk of fatal injury but also prosecution under relevant provisions of the Railways Act. The awareness component of the drive is focused on educating residents in neighbourhoods adjacent to the rail line, where shortcuts through track areas remain common despite repeated warnings.

The Bengaluru division's campaign forms part of a broader nationwide push by Indian Railways to reduce fatalities linked to trespassing, a problem that has historically outpaced even rail accidents in its human toll. While the 2,311-case tally covers only a two-month period, officials have indicated that sustained vigilance will be required to bring the numbers down. Railway Protection Force personnel and station masters have been instructed to escalate reporting and intervene wherever pedestrians are observed crossing or walking along the tracks.

Public cooperation has been identified as a critical factor in the drive's success, with authorities urging commuters, schools and resident welfare associations to amplify the safety messaging. Officials have also pointed to infrastructure-level interventions, including the closure of vulnerable entry points and the construction of foot overbridges, as longer-term measures complementing the current enforcement and awareness push in Bengaluru.
