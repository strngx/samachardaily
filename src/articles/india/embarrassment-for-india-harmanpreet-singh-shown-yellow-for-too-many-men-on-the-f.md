In a high‑profile clash against Belgium, the Indian men’s hockey team found itself under the spotlight for the wrong reasons. Midway through the second half, match officials stopped play and pointed out that twelve Indian players were on the field – one more than the eleven‑player limit stipulated by the International Hockey Federation (FIH). The breach prompted the referee to issue a yellow card to the team’s captain, Harmanpreet Singh, for a technical infringement. As per the rules, a yellow card for too many men on the field carries a temporary suspension, and Singh was required to leave the pitch for a five‑minute period while the side corrected the lineup.

The incident unfolded during a tightly contested encounter in which both sides were vying for a place in the knockout stages of the tournament. India, already under pressure to secure a win, saw its momentum disrupted as the extra player was identified and the captain, a key play‑maker, was forced to the bench. The team quickly reorganised, but the interruption highlighted a lapse in on‑field discipline and communication among the coaching staff and players. After the five‑minute suspension, the Indian side resumed with the correct number of players, but the episode left a sour note on an otherwise competitive performance.

Post‑match, Indian hockey officials acknowledged the mistake, describing it as a “rare oversight” and promising a review of substitution protocols. The incident sparked a flurry of reactions on social media, with fans and analysts labeling the episode an embarrassment for a nation that prides itself on its hockey heritage. While the final score remained close, the focus shifted from the on‑field tactics to the procedural error that cost the team valuable playing time and momentum.
