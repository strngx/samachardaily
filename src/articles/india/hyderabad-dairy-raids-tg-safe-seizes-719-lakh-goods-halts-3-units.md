In a major regulatory enforcement drive against food adulteration, food safety teams operating under Telangana's TG SAFE task force seized adulterated dairy products worth ₹7.19 lakh during coordinated raids across Hyderabad. The crackdown was launched following targeted intelligence regarding the illicit production and distribution of substandard dairy goods across the metropolitan region, prompting immediate physical inspections of processing centers and storage facilities.

A primary focus of the operation involved unannounced inspections at local manufacturing units, leading food safety officers to order the immediate shutdown of three facilities. Official inspectors discovered that these units were producing analogue paneer made with palm oil substitutes rather than authentic milk solids. Consequently, operations at all three processing plants were completely halted to stop the illegal distribution of mock paneer to retailers, restaurants, and consumers.

Alongside the operational shutdowns of the non-compliant units, food safety authorities also served formal notices to prominent distributor Mother Dairy. The regulatory notices require the entity to clarify quality compliance parameters and address specific findings noted by inspection teams during their citywide review. TG SAFE officials reiterated that established corporate brands are subject to the exact same rigorous safety protocols as independent local manufacturers.

Dairy adulteration, particularly the substitution of natural milk fats with cheap palm oil analogues in paneer and cheese, poses significant regulatory challenges across commercial food chains. Such unauthorized modifications lower manufacturing costs for unethical producers while deceiving consumers who expect genuine dairy items. Food safety task forces have systematically stepped up surprise visits to urban markets to ensure that high-demand staples meet statutory purity standards.

Following the seizure of the ₹7.19 lakh worth of illicit stock, sample batches were secured by TG SAFE inspectors to undergo comprehensive laboratory testing. Authorities indicated that formal legal actions, statutory penalties, and potential prosecution under food safety laws will follow once final analytical reports confirm the extent of the adulteration across the raided units and distribution channels.
