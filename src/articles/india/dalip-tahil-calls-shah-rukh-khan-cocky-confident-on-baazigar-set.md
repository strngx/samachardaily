In a candid interview with NDTV, veteran character actor Dalip Tahil said Shah Rukh Khan exuded a swagger he described as ‘Delhi harkatein’ during the shooting of the 1993 thriller Baazigar, where Tahil himself portrayed the antagonist Madan Chopra.

Tahil recalled that the young Khan entered the set with a “cocky and confident” demeanor, a trait he linked to the bustling energy of Delhi’s street culture. “He was full of Delhi harkatein – the bravado, the quick wit, the swagger you see on the streets,” Tahil said, adding that the confidence helped Khan deliver the film’s iconic anti‑hero performance.

Baazigar, directed by the Abbas‑Mustan duo, marked a turning point in Khan’s career, establishing him as a bankable lead capable of playing morally ambiguous roles. The film’s success hinged on its tight narrative, memorable music, and the chemistry among its cast, which included Kajol, Shilpa Shetty and, of course, Tahil’s Madan Chopra, a ruthless businessman who becomes the story’s primary foil.

Industry insiders noted that Tahil’s remarks shed light on the collaborative atmosphere of early‑1990s Bollywood, where emerging stars often learned on the job from seasoned actors. Fans of the film have taken to social media, debating whether Khan’s confidence was a natural trait or a performance strategy that later defined his on‑screen persona.

Tahil concluded by saying that witnessing Khan’s early confidence reminded him of the unpredictable nature of stardom, noting that the actor’s boldness on Baazigar foreshadowed the larger-than-life image he would later cultivate across Indian cinema.
