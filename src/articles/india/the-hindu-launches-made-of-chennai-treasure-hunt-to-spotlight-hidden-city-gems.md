The Hindu has announced the Made of Chennai treasure hunt, a city‑wide trail designed to lead participants to hidden cultural gems across the metropolis. The initiative encourages groups of friends to explore the capital of Tamil Nadu beyond the usual tourist hotspots, turning everyday streets into a game of discovery.

Chennai, long celebrated for its ancient temples, colonial architecture, bustling markets and coastal heritage, houses countless sites that remain off the mainstream radar. By weaving these locations into a structured hunt, the newspaper aims to bring lesser‑known landmarks into public view, offering a fresh perspective on the city’s layered history.

The treasure hunt operates on a clue‑based format, where participants receive a series of hints that point them toward successive locations. Each stop reveals a piece of Chennai’s cultural narrative, prompting participants to engage with the environment, photograph discoveries and share experiences on social media, thereby creating a collective map of the city’s hidden treasures.

Organisers say the event reflects The Hindu’s broader commitment to community engagement and cultural preservation. By leveraging its readership base, the newspaper hopes to foster a sense of ownership among citizens, encouraging them to protect and promote the sites they encounter during the hunt.

Open to the public, the Made of Chennai treasure hunt welcomes families, students, tourists and anyone eager to explore the city in a collaborative, interactive manner. The organisers anticipate strong participation, noting that the blend of adventure and education could set a precedent for future cultural initiatives in Indian metros.
