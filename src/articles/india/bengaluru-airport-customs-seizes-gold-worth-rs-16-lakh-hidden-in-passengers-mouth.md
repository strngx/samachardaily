Customs officials at Bengaluru's Kempegowda International Airport (KIA) intercepted an unusual attempt to smuggle precious metals after catching an incoming international passenger carrying gold inside his mouth. The passenger, who had landed in Karnataka on a flight originating from Abu Dhabi, was found carrying 100 grams of gold valued at approximately Rs 16 lakh. Officers flagged the traveler during arrival checks before discovering the concealed bullion.

The physical concealment method underscores the extreme tactics being employed to bypass international border security and tariff checkpoints. According to authorities, the passenger managed to board the flight in the United Arab Emirates with the metal stashed directly inside his oral cavity, attempting to clear Indian customs unnoticed upon arrival.

This successful recovery is far from an isolated event at the terminal. It represents the sixth distinct gold smuggling case uncovered by enforcement officers at Bengaluru's international airport within the span of just one week. The quick succession of seizures points to a surge in illicit transport along the aviation corridor connecting the Middle East to South India.

The repeated interceptions have prompted customs officials at KIA to significantly increase surveillance on high-volume routes originating from the UAE. Persistent price differentials and import duties between the Gulf region and India continue to drive smuggling operations, leading border agencies to step up passenger profiling and targeted physical inspections.

Following the recovery of the 100-gram gold stash, customs officers impounded the contraband worth Rs 16 lakh and initiated formal legal proceedings against the passenger. The case highlights ongoing operational challenges for airport border security as enforcement teams adapt to rapidly changing concealment methods.
