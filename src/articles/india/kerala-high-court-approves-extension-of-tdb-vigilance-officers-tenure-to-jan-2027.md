The Kerala High Court on Thursday formally confirmed the state’s request to prolong the tenure of the Tourism Development Board’s (TDB) Chief Vigilance and Security Officer (CVSO) until January 2027. The extension was filed by the state government, which argued that continuity in the board’s anti‑corruption and security apparatus is essential for the effective supervision of its tourism projects.

The CVSO heads the internal vigilance cell that monitors procurement, contract awards, and security protocols across TDB’s portfolio of hotels, resorts, and promotional initiatives. The role, created under the Kerala Vigilance Act, carries statutory authority to investigate irregularities, recommend corrective action, and liaise with the state’s anti‑corruption bureau. The incumbent, whose name was not disclosed in the court filing, has been in post since 2022 and has overseen several high‑value infrastructure contracts.

The court’s order comes amid a broader push by the Kerala administration to tighten governance in public‑sector undertakings. Over the past year, the state has faced scrutiny over alleged cost overruns in tourism‑related projects and calls for greater transparency. By extending the CVSO’s term, officials aim to avoid a leadership vacuum that could disrupt ongoing investigations and weaken internal controls.

Legal experts note that the High Court’s endorsement signals judicial support for the state’s proactive stance on vigilance. “The court’s decision reinforces the principle that continuity in oversight functions is a matter of public interest, especially in sectors that generate significant revenue and attract foreign investment,” said a senior advocate familiar with the case. The extension also aligns with the board’s five‑year strategic plan, which targets a 15 % increase in tourist arrivals by 2028.

The state has pledged to conduct periodic performance reviews of the CVSO and to submit quarterly reports to the High Court’s monitoring committee. Should any lapses be identified, the court retains the authority to order remedial measures or appoint an interim officer.
