On Monday evening in Bishkek, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Russian President Vladimir Putin were seen stepping out of the same car to attend the opening ceremony of the World Nomad Games at Bishkek Arena. The joint appearance came immediately after the two leaders wrapped up bilateral discussions on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit held in Kyrgyzstan’s capital. Their shared ride, a repeat of a similar gesture at the SCO Leaders’ Summit in Tianjin last September, was highlighted by analysts as a vivid display of what has been dubbed “car diplomacy.”

The SCO summit, which brings together China, Russia, India and four Central Asian states, provided the backdrop for the leaders’ talks on regional security, trade and multilateral cooperation. The earlier Tianjin episode, where Modi and Putin also travelled together to a bilateral meeting, sparked extensive commentary on social media platforms worldwide, emphasizing the symbolic weight of leaders sharing a vehicle in diplomatic protocol. Observers noted that such gestures can convey a sense of personal rapport that transcends formal statecraft.

During their Bishkek meeting, Modi reiterated India’s stance on the war in Ukraine, calling for an end to the conflict and describing peace as essential for humanity’s well‑being. He said, “Every day spent in war pushes humanity backwards, while every step towards peace fills humanity with new hope.” The Indian premier framed the appeal as a “call of humanity,” invoking the shared legacy of Gandhi and Buddha as a moral compass for both nations. Putin, in turn, acknowledged India’s consistent support for peace initiatives and expressed willingness to continue dialogue on the issue.

Earlier in the week, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar met Putin in Moscow, delivering a personal message from Modi and co‑chairing the 27th session of the India‑Russia Inter‑Governmental Commission on Trade, Economic, Scientific, Technological and Cultural Cooperation (IRIGC‑TEC). The Kremlin’s statement highlighted a rise in bilateral trade turnover this year after a slight dip last year, crediting Modi’s personal attention to the relationship. Discussions covered energy, fertilizer supplies for Indian agriculture, nuclear cooperation and high‑tech collaboration, with both sides indicating steady progress on these fronts.

Looking ahead, Modi announced that India will host the BRICS summit in Delhi next month, extending a warm invitation to Putin and the Russian delegation. He said the upcoming summit would further strengthen bilateral ties and deepen cooperation within the BRICS framework. The invitation underscores the strategic importance both capitals place on maintaining a robust partnership amid shifting global alignments.
