Stranded Maharashtra citizens who had been caught in Nepal's severe flooding expressed gratitude to state and central government agencies for coordinating their rescue and repatriation.

## What happened

Pilgrims and travellers from districts including Kolhapur, Pune, and Mumbai were stranded in Kathmandu and eastern Nepal following flash floods that disrupted road and air transport links. According to the Maharashtra Chief Minister's Office (CMO) and reporting by Press Trust of India (PTI), the rescue operation involved coordinated efforts among the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu, the Nepal government's disaster management apparatus, and Maharashtra's State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA).

Indian Embassy personnel facilitated passage for stranded citizens through a mix of road convoys and chartered air transfers. Emergency supplies including food and medical assistance were provided at relief camps.

## Gratitude and virtual debrief

During a virtual interaction hosted by state authorities, dozens of rescued Maharashtra citizens thanked the CMO, state government, and central government for the "rapid coordination and constant support" throughout the crisis. State leadership conducted a virtual debrief with returnees to document the sequence of rescue events.

## Why it matters

The episode demonstrated the operational framework of India's cross-border emergency consular response. The Maharashtra government's visibility in managing the crisis — including the direct involvement of the CMO — reinforced public confidence in state-level disaster management capacity, particularly in situations where Indian nationals abroad are affected by natural disasters in neighbouring countries.
