Investigations across Pakistan, India, the United States, and Canada revealed illicit networks operating in healthcare facilities, mortuaries, and burial grounds. In Punjab, law enforcement dismantled organ-harvesting rings after rescuing victims from makeshift surgical units. In major Indian metropolitan hubs, undercover probes exposed attendants secretly removing tissues and organs from deceased bodies before families received them.

In the United States, a multi-state tissue-harvesting syndicate illegally extracted bones, skin, and organs from hundreds of corpses at funeral homes without family consent, forging authorization papers to sell materials to supply companies. In Canada, over 20 grieving families reported missing memorial items—lanterns, potted plants, and personal tributes—after a cemetery theft investigation recovered dozens of stolen keepsakes.

Authorities in Jammu & Kashmir report no recorded cases of institutional organ theft, mortuary desecration, or organized grave-rooing. Officials enforce strict licensing under the Transplantation of Human Organs Act and advise the public against viral hoaxes about hospital organ theft during health emergencies.
